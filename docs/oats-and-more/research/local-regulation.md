# Boise / Ada County: vending location and food-operation research

**Research notes updated November 11, 2026. NOT a permit or legal opinion.** Read the actual code, confirm the latest version and have regulators review the **specific parcel and proposed unit type**. Boise, unincorporated Ada County and nearby incorporated cities are **not** interchangeable jurisdictions.

## Site model being considered

One **hosted private commercial lot or office campus**, serving breakfast about **6:30–9:30 a.m.** from a small portable counter or lightweight towable station. Host agrees to welcome the operation and provide **electricity at an approved location**. This contrasts with **street/sidewalk vending** on public rights-of-way, which can have different hours, licenses, positioning and utility-connection limitations.

**The crucial distinction:** property-owner permission grants only what the owner legally controls. The sidewalk and even a landscaped strip may be inside the public right-of-way. Survey or ACHD property/ROW records are needed for a borderline site.

## Boise city — authority and issues

### 1. Public sidewalk and street vending

The Boise vendor law (Title 3, Chapter 2) includes placement, visibility, pedestrian passage, intersection/doorway/driveway constraints and special downtown limitations. The 2026 amendment to **section 3-2-12** is especially relevant.

- **Hours:** the current amendment provides a **6:00 a.m.** start in specified **commercially zoned** areas on the listed weekdays, compared with the generally later 9:00 a.m. start outside them. **Confirm the exact proposed location and applicable hours before assuming a breakfast cart is allowed.**
- Vending does not grant exclusive rights to a public sidewalk/street site; event and park permission may be separate.
- **Utilities:** Boise's vendor restrictions include **public-way utility connection prohibitions** and electrical-cord restrictions. A host's outlet across the sidewalk should **not** be treated as an acceptable workaround.
- Other requirements discussed: clear pedestrian path, avoidance of doorways/driveways/crosswalks and corner areas, and trash control; verify every measurement in the *currently applicable law* instead of designing from memory.

Sources:
- Boise's **2026 ordinance 29-26**, amendments to vendors (primary legal text): https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/boise_id/latest/boise/0-0-0-88829
- General vendor restrictions, Boise City Code 3-2-12: https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/boise_id/latest/boise/0-0-0-1673
- Vendor definitions, Boise City Code Title 3: https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/boise_id/latest/boise/0-0-0-1588

The online code itself cautions that the publication may lag current legislation. Contact Boise City Clerk for an authoritative current interpretation.

### 2. On private commercial property

Boise zoning has a category for **accessory food trucks**, with conditions such as:
- Written permission from the owner describing the approved placement.
- Avoid obstructing required parking, access, drive aisles and pedestrian movement.
- Keep appropriately sized trash receptacles and remove waste.
- No vehicle drive-through service from the truck.

A distinct **temporary mobile food truck** provision can limit qualifying temporary use to **7 consecutive days** and cap occupation of required parking at **10%**, subject to specific permits/exceptions. Do not assume this is the only route for repeated daily hosting or that a small pop-up counter automatically qualifies the same way as a “food truck.” **The City must classify the actual setup** (portable kiosk vs open trailer vs enclosed concession vehicle) and confirm whether recurring use is allowed for the host's parcel.

Source:
- Boise zoning code and Food Truck, Accessory standards (see 11-03-03.4(F)): https://www.cityofboise.org/media/20188/2025-boise-zoning-code-effective-july-1-2025.pdf
- Temporary-use provisions (published modification): https://www.cityofboise.org/media/20188/2025-boise-zoning-code-modification-june-30-2025-2.pdf

### 3. Right-of-way / ACHD

The **Ada County Highway District** manages much of the public roadway/sidewalk right-of-way. For any proposed placement near an office complex curb or sidewalk, confirm **where the private parcel ends and the public right-of-way begins**; a landlord saying “yes” is not sufficient for placement in the ROW.

Official ACHD right-of-way map service (mapping aid, not substitute for official determination): https://gis.achdidaho.org/server/rest/services/ArcGIS_Hub/Official_ROW/MapServer

Action: contact ACHD Right of Way and City Clerk with a site plan and photos.

## Unincorporated Ada County and other cities

Ada County outside city limits requires its own **parcel-specific zoning and temporary-use classification**. Certain planned communities have specific food-vendor provisions (historical research referenced Avimor and Spring Rock, but those rules cannot be generalized countywide).

- First identify city limits / jurisdiction.
- Then determine parcel zoning, permanent vs temporary use and parking/access requirements.
- For sites in Meridian, Garden City, Eagle, Kuna or Star, use **that city's rules**, not Boise's.
- Health requirements may still run through **Central District Health** throughout the relevant service area.

## Central District Health (CDH) — before buying or modifying equipment

CDH regulates mobile food operations that handle TCS foods (food requiring time/temperature control for safety). **Cooked hot oatmeal** and any refrigerated toppings should be evaluated with CDH. A simple menu can help, but does **not** itself prove exemption.

Key items from CDH's published materials:
- Mobile food unit license/application and operational plan review **before operation**.
- **Submit plan review at least 30 days in advance**; new builds or major modifications may require longer.
- Submit proposed **menu, commissary or approved prep arrangements, layout, equipment list and food flow**. Get plan approval **before construction/remodel**.
- Arrange **pre-operational inspection** in advance; application packet suggests notifying the district at least **15 days** before opening.
- Verify sanitary handwash water, wastewater disposal, protected toppings, approved warewashing/utensil processes, temperature monitoring and TCS hot holding.
- Hot holding generally uses **135°F or above** per food-code guidance; any specific alternative time-as-public-health-control procedure must be approved as applicable.
- Many electric soup kettles **only hold already-hot food**. Ask whether the preparation site is approved and the reheating/transport step is lawful.
- Allergen prevention and communication for **walnuts, pecans and peanut products** are a real operational consideration.
- CDH's current public page lists **$100** plan review, and example full-service mobile license fee **$80** (licensed or no commissary category) / **$100** (unlicensed commissary category). **Confirm fees/category at application; a portable stand may be classified differently.**
- CDH notes the need for at least one certified food protection manager associated with a food establishment; clarify applicability for the proposed entity/operator.

Official CDH:
- Food establishments, applications/fees: https://cdh.idaho.gov/licenses-permits-inspections/food-establishments/
- Mobile food operational plan: https://cdh.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Mobile-Food-Operational-Plan.pdf
- CDH main contact: **(208) 375-5211**

Idaho recently revised its direct-to-consumer food law; **do not extrapolate “cottage food” allowances to an unreviewed hot oatmeal operation**. Clarify the mobile unit and kitchen requirements directly with CDH.

## Electrical and fire/site safety

- Assess total wattage of warmers under simultaneous heating duty, plus point-of-sale devices and potential lighting.
- Confirm dedicated/adequately rated electrical supply with host and applicable electrical officials (as needed).
- Weather-safe, approved cords/connections with **no pedestrian trip path**; never run cords over public sidewalks.
- Verify canopy/wind anchoring and food-service protections; if propane ever enters the design, investigate fire code separately.
- Plan offsite washing, waste transport and equipment sanitizing; host outlet alone doesn't make a unit compliant.

## Tow/vehicle legality

An ordinary 5×8 enclosed trailer is often impractical given the 2017 Rogue rating. Use 1,000 lb loaded trailer / 100 lb tongue as **conservative preliminary design boundaries**, based on Nissan's published 2017 specifications, while the owner's manual lists 1,100 / 110 under specific assumptions.

- Nissan published specs: https://usa.nissannews.com/en-US/releases/2017-nissan-rogue-specifications
- Owner manual: https://www.nissanusa.com/content/dam/Nissan/us/manuals-and-guides/rogue/2017/2017-Nissan-Rogue-owner-manual.pdf

Trailer selection also requires confirming hitch receiver, trailer brakes (if required), lighting, registration, manufacturer GVWR/GAWR, real trailer and tongue scale weights, load distribution and the Rogue's allowed vehicle payload.

## Questions to send to authorities (actual location/vehicle drawing required)

**Boise City Clerk / Planning**
1. Is a heated, ready-to-serve **portable counter** operated every weekday on this specific private commercial lot permitted? What does Boise classify it as?
2. If instead on a **light open utility trailer**, does it qualify as an accessory food truck, temporary use or other category?
3. Are 6:30–9:30 a.m. sales permitted here? Any site-specific zoning, off-street parking, sidewalk, accessibility or noise requirements?
4. What written host approvals, licenses, site plans, restrictions on number of days, electrical access, and setbacks are needed?

**CDH**
1. For **plain hot oatmeal + dry toppings + occasional fruit/peanut/nut add-ons**, what exact permit, commissary, certified manager, prep and hot-holding configuration is required?
2. Can approved externally cooked oatmeal be transported to two electric warmers; what temperature, transfer and documentation rules apply?
3. What handwashing, water tank, wastewater, food-protection and utensil-cleaning provisions are needed for the smallest acceptable unit?
4. Can we test a single morning with the proposed configuration, and what process would authorize that?

**ACHD / landowner**
1. Show exact legal ROW boundary, pedestrian path, driveway/access clearance and plug-in location.
2. Confirm written permission covers the land actually used and does not interfere with required parking or ADA access.

## Risk register (unresolved)

- **High:** buying a trailer/unit that cannot be permitted or towed.
- **High:** cooking/reheating oatmeal in a warmer only rated for holding.
- **High:** assuming a public-sidewalk host outlet or permission is legally usable.
- **Medium:** heavy arrival waves, spilling/handling a hot product, water/handwashing logistics.
- **Medium:** winter exposure, spoilage and loss of consistent host access.
