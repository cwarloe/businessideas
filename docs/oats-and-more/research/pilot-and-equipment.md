# Oats, Etc. — menu, volume, vending format and gross-sales model

**Captured November 11, 2026. All prices/volumes are illustrative until tested.**
Single operator, oatmeal breakfast first, one recurring location, power supplied by a cooperating host where legally permitted.

## What are we actually selling?

**Stage 1:** familiar, hot, comforting oatmeal, finished quickly with dry/shelf-stable toppings. **Egg is excluded from the initial menu.** Do not design the pilot around a fryer, grill, egg station, coffee bar, or several independently cooked grains.

Potential 3-bowl starter menu (working price points):
- **Classic Brown Sugar & Cinnamon — $5.95**
- **Maple Walnut — $7.50**
- **Apple Pie Oats — $7.95** (apple topping handling / storage subject to recipe and CDH review)

Suggested optional add-ons (example upcharge, **not** verified demand or margins):
- Cinnamon + brown sugar + raisins: **+$0.75** (can be part of standard bowl).
- Walnuts or pecans: **+$1.50**; keep nut allergens distinctly labeled and managed.
- Maple/nut/crunch bundle: **+$2.00**.
- Extra oatmeal/large size: **+$2.00**; consumes capacity, so price and availability must reflect that.
- Shelf-stable individually packaged drink: **+$2.00–$3.00** if storage and waste are manageable.
- **10-bowl team pack**: price per bowl, useful for preordering and reducing uncertainty, not a “buy ten get one free” margin assumption.

Avoid immediately creating 20 variations. The simplest version uses **one unsweetened cooked oatmeal base**, with a few toppings assembled to order; this allows demand substitution without wasting three preflavored recipes. Consider preportioning selected toppings to limit counter time. Other grains can wait.

**Stage 2 breakfast additions:** Pecan Raisin Crunch, Banana & Peanut Butter, maybe a single alternate grain **only if demand validates it**. Dry toppings and portion increments precede more equipment. Coffee is a plausible upsell but requires another preparation/electrical workflow and should be tested later.

**Stage 3 lunch additions (not part of current operating design):** Mushroom & Herb Savory Oats, Southwest Bowl with beans/corn/peppers, and Cheddar & Herb Grits; incorporate rice or cream of wheat only if demand and prep/storage permit. These have different temperature-control and cross-contamination considerations. Aim for overlapping ingredients and a few approved stations.

## The three-stage development path

| Stage | Offer | Form factor / site | Hypothetical gross sales |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Validate** | One oatmeal base, three recognizable recipes, fast service | One hosted pop-up breakfast counter/kiosk; Rogue transports modular equipment | **20–35 bowls/day**, 3 days/week, **$7.50** average: **$1,949–$3,410/month** |
| **2 · Establish** | 4–5 breakfast recipes, clear upsells, preorder/office bundles | Reliable site and upgraded serving counter; trailer only if ROI and towing allow | **40–60 bowls/day**, 4 days/week, **$8** average: **$5,542–$8,314/month** |
| **3 · Expand** | Breakfast plus 2–3 savory lunch bowls; occasional grouped orders | Better-equipped trailer or fixed site; perhaps second operator/location eventually | **65–95 bowls/day**, 5 days/week, **$9.50** average: **$13,367–$19,540/month** |

*Monthly figures = bowls/day × average ticket × days/week × 4.33. These are **model inputs**, not forecasts or profits. Earlier Stage 1 thinking was 30 bowls by 9 AM, but the current site hypothesis is potentially 40–60 sold over a three-hour breakfast window. Do not make 30 a hard ceiling if a large office site proves higher demand.*

## Hot-holding capacity: actual math

One U.S. quart = **4 cups = 32 U.S. fluid ounces**. The following uses **80% fill** as an *illustrative working volume* to leave headroom and permit safe handling; actual usable volume depends on the model, food consistency and approved operating practices. Bowl size is the **cooked oatmeal portion**, not the printed cup volume including toppings.

| Number × nominal kettle capacity | Nominal total | 80% working volume | 8 fl oz servings | 10 fl oz servings | 12 fl oz servings |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **3 × 5 qt** | 15 qt | 48 cups | 48 | 38 | 32 |
| **4 × 5 qt** | 20 qt | 64 cups | 64 | 51 | 42 |
| **2 × 11 qt** | 22 qt | 70.4 cups | 70 | 56 | 46 |
| **3 × 11 qt** | 33 qt | 105.6 cups | 105 | 84 | 70 |
| **2 × 14 qt** | 28 qt | 89.6 cups | 89 | 71 | 59 |

Counts are floored, before spills and leftovers. Your eventual bowl fill may be 8–10 fl oz cooked oatmeal plus toppings; test perceived value and actual finished mass. For a 50–60-bowl morning, **two 11-quart kettles** offer useful capacity *if* the oatmeal component is approximately 8 ounces (70 theoretical) or a 10-ounce portion with a modest remainder (56 theoretical). A third vessel or approved restocking method increases buffer.

**Important:** Many “soup kettles” are **hot-holding appliances**, *not* cookers or rapid reheaters. Plan to prepare and reheat (if applicable) using a CDH-approved process and approved preparation space, then hold safely in equipment confirmed by the manufacturer. Validate heating times, temperature distribution and approved transfer/loading procedure. Do **not** assume you may cook at home and bring it to a licensed cart.

### Electricity and circuit budget

- Earlier 5-quart commercial warmer example: **500 W each**, so 3 units can be **1,500 W combined** (if those exact units). At 120 V that's **12.5 A**, before other electrical loads. Rated power is **not** the same as measured continuous energy usage.
- Illustrative 11-quart example discussed: **400 W each**; 2 units would be **800 W** and 3 units **1,200 W**, **if confirmed by the specific product data plate**.
- A host-provided outlet must be **adequate for the full load on its actual circuit**, including any other devices, safe cord routing, outdoor electrical protection, and local electrical compliance.
- **Do not route extension cords across a public sidewalk.** Boise's vendor restrictions also address public-right-of-way utility connections. Private site access is key, and permission does not waive code requirements.
- Stop planning around a giant e-bike battery or generator for a stationary 3-hour shift. A powered host station changes the design economics.

## Mobility concept comparison

| Option | Advantages | Constraints | Working status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vending e-bike / 3-wheel trike** | Charming marketing, bicycle mobility, relatively small visual footprint | Specialized purchase, limited payload/storage, transport battery not a kitchen power supply | **Alternative**, less compelling with one fixed hosted location |
| **Portable kiosk transported inside Rogue** | No trailer/hitch, low initial spend, modular, easiest to test location | Setup/teardown effort; need compliant weather/food protection | **Preferred pilot to investigate** |
| **Very light 4×6 or 4×8 open utility trailer with counter** | Trailer arrives loaded; less unloading; can be branded | Payload, tongue weight, weather and washing setup must be designed carefully | **Secondary pilot candidate** |
| **5×8 enclosed trailer / 6×10 concession trailer** | Attractive cottage/shop styling, more secure counter, improved service | Empty trailer can nearly use or exceed Rogue towing limits; food fit-out adds mass and expense | **Defer until towing capability/operating needs justify** |
| **Future little green “oatmeal house” trailer** | Signature portable storefront/marketing asset | Not a substitute for code-compliant chassis, equipment, food-safe finishes or towing limits | **Stage 2/3 brand concept**, not a purchase recommendation now |

Past **rough comparison leads** (advertised examples and ranges, not live confirmed Boise quotes):
- Commercial Ferla-style vending e-bike around **$3,500–$6,500+** before foodservice customization.
- Bare 5×8 enclosed utility trailer example around **$3,495**, about **855 lb empty**, meaning very little headroom with the Rogue.
- 6×10 concession shell around **$8,000+**; some built-out examples **$18,000**. Usually too heavy for this tow vehicle.
- Light 4×8 folding utility trailer example around **249 lb**, or 4×6 aluminum around **280 lb**. Specific axle/frame ratings and food-safe modifications must be checked.
- A folding counter/canopy transported in the SUV can be cheaper initially than either specialty bike or finished trailer, but **no actual compliant total installed cost is known**.

Avoid making purchases based on those price snippets; get current quotations including materials, food-grade surfaces, handwashing/wastewater, rain/wind resistance and registration.

## 2017 Nissan Rogue tow constraint

**Strong reason not to buy a normal concession trailer yet.**

Nissan publishes two close figures:
- **2017 Rogue owner manual**: maximum towing **1,100 lb**, maximum tongue **110 lb**, subject to occupants/cargo/options.
- **2017 Nissan Rogue technical specifications**: properly equipped towing **1,000 lb**, tongue **100 lb**.

Use **1,000 / 100 lb conservatively** pending exact trim, FWD/AWD, installed equipment, load, tire information, hitch, and vehicle-specific confirmation. Neither figure is a promise a particular setup is safe.

Example *only*, not weighed: an ultra-light trailer at 250 lb + counters/canopy at 120 + warmers at 100 + oatmeal at 50 + toppings/packaging at 50 + water/washing at 50 + other at 30 = **650 lb** loaded. The *real* sanitary system, required water/wastewater storage, roof, brakes and weatherproofing could greatly raise this. Tongue-weight distribution must be verified and a trailer scale used.

Nissan official specs: https://usa.nissannews.com/en-US/releases/2017-nissan-rogue-specifications  
Owner's manual: https://www.nissanusa.com/content/dam/Nissan/us/manuals-and-guides/rogue/2017/2017-Nissan-Rogue-owner-manual.pdf

**Outstanding questions:** Does the Rogue already have hitch and wiring? What trim/drivetrain and allowable capacity does the specific vehicle show? What is the actual gross loaded weight and tongue load? How will setup be secured against movement/tipping? Can the operator manage setup safely alone?

## What must be proven before upgrading

- Preorders/presales and a willing office host, with a realistic 6:30–9:30 a.m. window.
- Bowl demand and repeat demand measured on **multiple dates**; not only an enthusiastic informal poll.
- Actual **ingredient, topping and packaging costs** and food wastage; packaging and nut costs may dominate extras.
- Whether 40–60 bowls are feasible for **one operator** during peak arrivals, not just averaged over a shift.
- Electrical capacity and **CDH-approved** food preparation, hot holding, water, handwashing, wastewater and transport.
- Vehicle fit/weight/tongue limits for the exact loaded design.
- A payoff calculation for trailer time saved versus complexity, upkeep, insurance, storage and towing.
