# Oats, Etc. · next decision brief

Prepared November 18, 2026. **Proposed discovery work. No interviews or test sales have been run for this brief.** Charles remains the decision owner. Charles or a named successor would conduct customer conversations and record evidence. The suggested timebox and sample below are working defaults for this round.

## Decision to unlock

Is a prepaid oatmeal breakfast for one office occasion worth pricing with an actual buyer and an available kitchen?

Start with the classic bowl on its own. The earlier $5.95 price is a hypothesis. An optional drink or topping can be priced after understanding the buyer's occasion. Keep a staffed counter and individual preorders eligible if buyer evidence favors them. The broader brand can still develop sweet and savory offers.

## Three uncertainties in priority order

| Assumption | Consequence if wrong | Least expensive next evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Someone already buying office breakfast would consider oatmeal for a real occasion | No reachable buyer at a workable price | Conversation about the last order with the person who selected or paid for it |
| That buyer can be served on time at an adequate full-service price | A paid order could consume too much work or kitchen cost | Actual requirements, then a written kitchen quote and timed process rehearsal |
| The occasion repeats often enough | One booking may fail to support continuing acquisition work | Normal calendar, purchase frequency and later full-price reorder opportunity |

## First round of discovery

**Suggested timebox** — two total hours for finding contacts, conversations and notes, then review. Aim to learn from three relevant buyers if they can be reached within the timebox. This is an exploratory sample. Log unsuccessful invitations and revise the recruitment route if access fails.

**Recruitment route** — begin with office administrators or team leaders who personally arranged a recent breakfast. One tenant at Interchange Business Complex is a lead from the prior research, subject to actual access. A known office contact may be an easier first conversation. Record existing relationships. Keep personal contact details private.

**Proposed invitation**

> I’m exploring an oatmeal breakfast service for office meetings. Could I ask about the last breakfast you arranged, how you chose it, and what worked or caused trouble? I’m using those details to decide whether to develop an offer.

Ask these questions in order.

1. Tell me about the last breakfast you organized. What was the occasion, date and headcount?
2. What did you order, from whom, and what was the total including delivery and other charges?
3. Who chose the food, approved the budget and handled payment?
4. What mattered most when deciding? What alternatives did you consider?
5. What happened during delivery, setup and cleanup? Were there leftovers or complaints?
6. When do you next expect to arrange breakfast? What would make you change providers?
7. After hearing about the existing process, describe a simple oatmeal option. Ask what would make it unsuitable, which dietary or packing requirements apply, and whether a priced proposal for a specific occasion would be useful.

## Decision at the end of the timebox

- **Advance to a priced proposal** when a qualified buyer identifies an actual occasion and agrees to review an offer with headcount, delivery window and budget requirements. This establishes an opportunity to quote.
- **Revise** when office breakfast is purchased but oatmeal, packaging, timing or access misses the need. Name the requirement that would change the offer.
- **Park this route** when the available evidence indicates no relevant occasion, no access, or requirements that cannot plausibly be met.
- **Inconclusive** when qualified buyers were not reached or responses conflict. Name the missing observation and the time needed for one follow-up.

Record raw counts and objections in a copy of the [run record](../../../playbook/discovery-and-test-run-template.md). A conversation cannot establish paid conversion or repeat demand.

## Before proposing a paid trial

Use the [working business plan](../../../playbook/business-plan-template.md) to enter one buyer's actual requirements, a landed food/packaging budget, complete kitchen terms and all operator time. Rehearse the whole job, including outreach and cleanup. Reprice for an ordinary delay and a smaller order. The operator's acceptable return is still open. Report the possible return, cash loss and cushion so the decision owner can choose the next commitment.

The earlier office model proposed 30 bowls and eight cocoa servings for $202.50 and left only a small time/cost cushion. Use its calculations as a starting scenario. Replace its assumptions before quoting the actual service. Identify the licensed operator, location and food process and resolve the requirements applicable to the proposed trial before accepting a commitment to serve.

**Review trigger** — after the suggested two-hour round, or sooner when evidence would change the decision. Set a calendar date when the person doing the work is assigned.
