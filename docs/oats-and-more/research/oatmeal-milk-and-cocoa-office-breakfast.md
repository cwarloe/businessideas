# Oats, Etc. with milk or hot chocolate at a Boise office

**Decision memo · November 18, 2026.** Explore a single employer-preordered oatmeal breakfast with one optional batch of hot chocolate. Keep the bowl available on its own. At 30 bowls and eight drinks, changing from a staffed counter to a short delivery increases modeled operator earnings from **$8.06 to $16.90 per hour**. Adding cocoa to the staffed counter increases earnings by only **$1.32 per hour**. One extra half hour on the delivery drops the return to **$14.79 per hour**. A quote for kitchen time and a timed delivery route matter more than refining the chocolate recipe at this stage.

## What the customer would buy

One water-cooked, unsweetened oatmeal base, about eight fluid ounces cooked in a 12 oz bowl. Include brown sugar, cinnamon and raisins as simple finishes. Offer one preportioned walnut topping at **+$1.50** if the site can manage nut cross-contact. The classic **$5.95** bowl comes from the existing Oats plan. Proposed add-ons are **$2 sealed milk**, **$2 plain hot milk**, or **$3 hot chocolate**. These are competing menu choices in the calculation. A bowl and cocoa together cost **$8.95 before tax**. Breakfast is the small initial service within the broader sweet-and-savory brand.

**For hot chocolate, the simplest Boise-specific version is warmed pasteurized chocolate milk.** Reed’s Boise ordering page lists a half gallon at $5. Model a seven-ounce pour into an eight-ounce hot cup, with eight saleable pours per half gallon and eight ounces allowed for residue or loss. Confirm that the product heats well, stays smooth and works with the proposed equipment and district-approved process. A scratch alternative is pasteurized whole milk and measured chocolate syrup. Hershey’s publishes a recipe using two tablespoons per cup of milk. A retail syrup price of $4.84 for 24 oz is a weight-based price. Weigh one test portion before pricing it per serving. Omit whipped cream, marshmallows and extra flavors from the first order. [5] [16] [17]

A splash of cold milk stirred into oatmeal is a fourth use with different economics. One fluid ounce from a $5.50 gallon costs **4.3 cents of milk** before loss, container or handling. A splash can add value to a bowl without selling another drink. Opening a gallon just for 30 one-ounce pours uses less than one-quarter of it. A small approved pack, a host's shared inventory with agreed costs, or omitting the splash can make more sense. Keep dairy optional and keep its utensils separate. [5]

## What remains for the operator

Assume 30 bowls prepared and sold, eight drink add-ons on the same card tickets, no paid walnut orders, and zero host revenue share. Two prime kitchen hours cost $80 using the Commons listing. Allow $20 for common shift costs. The operator works 6.5 hours for bowls alone, including three hours selling, two hours of kitchen work and 1.5 hours for the rest. Hot drinks add $3 and half an hour. Sealed milk adds six minutes. All allowances and labor times are estimates. Income tax, costs above the allowances and equipment recovery reduce the figures. [8] [9] [18] [19]

| Staffed breakfast menu | Cash left before operator pay | Hours | Cash per total hour | Extra cash after valuing the added time at $16.46/hr |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Bowls alone | $43.83 | 6.5 | $6.74 | — |
| Plus eight sealed milks | $49.40 | 6.6 | $7.49 | $3.93 |
| Plus eight plain hot milks | $50.92 | 7.0 | $7.27 | −$1.14 |
| Plus eight hot chocolates | $56.45 | 7.0 | $8.06 | $4.39 |

The local wage comparison is the BLS May 2025 Boise metro average for food preparation and serving. The owner specified no earnings target. If someone takes this over independently, the cash left after bills is theirs to compensate their time and investment. If they are hired, budget actual wages and employer costs and count the owner's supervision separately. [20]

**Cocoa has a minimum useful batch.** Each attached $3 sale adds $2.58 after the incremental card percentage and a $0.34 cup/lid, before the batch charge. The whole batch costs $5 even if half remains, plus $3 shift expense and $8.23 for the extra half hour at the comparison wage. Four sales cover the $8 cash expense but leave just $2.31 for the extra half hour. **Seven paid cups cover the batch, extra cash costs and the comparison wage**, equivalent to 23% of 30 bowl orders. At eight cups the cushion is only $4.39. A 15% host share raises the minimum to eight cups. A second batch incurs another $5, and higher volumes may add labor. A bowl lost to a slow drink line costs about $4.79 of contribution, enough to consume the first batch’s cushion.

**A paid topping deserves equal attention.** Half an ounce of walnuts costs about $0.31 at the observed price. A $1.50 upgrade on the same ticket adds about **$1.15** after nuts and card percentage, before portioning, loss or host share. Eleven upgrades add about **$12.62**, roughly the entire cash improvement from eight hot chocolates. Test uptake and allergen handling before counting that money. [15] [18]

The $120 case assumes four hours must be purchased at Flava's posted $30/hour and only two hours of kitchen labor are needed. If the operator actually works all four, divide $16.45 by nine hours. At the $80 kitchen price it takes **43 bowls** with eight drinks, all prepared bowls sold, to clear that wage comparison. [9] [10] [20]

### A single employer order changes the result

At the individual menu prices, an employer could order **30 bowls and eight hot chocolates for $202.50 before tax**. One online card invoice costs about $7.38 including processing on tax. Thirty individual in-person tickets would cost about $10.08. Keeping the $80 kitchen and other cash assumptions, the group order leaves **$59.14** for the operator and costs beyond the allowances. The advantage comes mainly from reclaiming selling time.

| Total work for the complete group order | Operator return | Consequence |
|---|---:|---|
| 3.5 hours | $16.90/hr | Requires two hours of kitchen work and only 90 minutes in total for ordering, sourcing, loading, travel, setup/handoff, washing and admin. |
| 4 hours | $14.79/hr | One extra half hour takes the operator below the local comparison. |
| 4.5 hours | $13.14/hr | An invoice near **$218** before tax would be needed to reach $16.46/hour at the same costs. Any additional fees raise that floor. |

At the proposed $202.50 invoice, **3 hours 36 minutes** is approximately the maximum all-in time to reach the wage comparison. The 3.5-hour case leaves only **$1.53** beyond that amount. Quote delivery, disposables, host charges, order-taking time and any equipment return trip explicitly. An employer commitment tests the value of a delivered breakfast. Individual willingness to buy a second drink another week is a separate question.

## Site, equipment and approval

**First location lead:** one tenant at Interchange Business Complex near Overland and Cole, with one named buyer and one delivery point. The manager describes eight buildings and more than 15 owner-occupants and tenants. Those figures give no reliable employee count or permission to vend. Selling across eight buildings would add travel, handoffs and time. Trailhead is a secondary candidate for a prepaid meeting, where the free coffee and snacks are already known. The Commons at 2513 S Federal Way is a prep lead. Verify early availability, minimum billing, storage and the route. Flava at 7609 W Overland is an alternative kitchen lead. [6]–[10]

**One operator and three five-quart warmers:** at the prior project's illustrative 80% fill, one warmer holds 128 fluid ounces. Two oatmeal warmers hold 32 eight-ounce portions or only 25 ten-ounce portions. Reassigning the third vessel to a beverage leaves very little buffer for 30 bowls and cannot supply the 45-bowl case without approved resupply. A soup warmer's food compatibility, temperature distribution and usable volume need confirmation. A dedicated five-liter Avantco example uses 1,000 W and is intended to hold and dispense premixed hot beverages. Adding it to three illustrative 500 W warmers produces 2,500 W of combined nameplate load. Check actual nameplates, dedicated circuits, the host's permission and the district's acceptance. A short approved delivery may avoid installing a beverage station in the office altogether. [24]

**Heating and serving change the approval question.** The prior Milk Bar inquiry described cold drinks. It does not establish approval for cooking oatmeal, heating milk, moving two hot products, or office catering. For the actual menu and location, identify who is the licensed operator and seller, where each product is prepared, who is responsible at delivery, whether the client self-serves, and whether any food returns to the kitchen. Ask CDH to classify employer-paid delivery, employee-prepaid pickups and recurring walk-ups separately. Submit the agreed process, site plan, approved handwashing/warewashing, hot-hold and transport equipment, and a time/temperature log. Verify city and property requirements for the chosen arrangement. No combined-menu inquiry was sent and no new site determination was obtained for this memo. [21]–[23]

For applicable time/temperature-controlled foods, CDH gives **135°F or above for hot holding** and **41°F or below for cold holding**. Include temperatures at kitchen departure, office arrival and handoff/end of service. Holding temperature is distinct from the cooking or reheating process the district accepts. For a first service, propose same-day preparation in an approved facility and disposal of remaining hot product. Price that waste. Verify whether a carrier or dispenser is suitable for dairy, retains safe temperatures at partial fill, cleans adequately, and delivers a pleasant drink. Keep milk and nuts clearly identified, use dedicated utensils and retain packaging. Test lid fit, safe transport and burn/spill control before any service. [21]–[24]

## Recommended next decision

1. **Check the office offer first.** Seek interest from one identifiable employer in 30 classic bowls at $5.95 and an optional eight-cup cocoa order at $24, plus any delivery charge required by the timed route. Record the buyer, date, expected headcount and whether the employer or employees pay. Interest and a paid repeat order are different evidence.
2. **Price and time the whole route.** Confirm the kitchen's booking block and cleanup terms. Time sourcing, setup, packing, delivery, sanitation and any return trip. If $202.50 buys a 4.5-hour commitment, reprice toward at least $218 or reduce time/cost before taking the order. Do not assign spare hours to the operator for free.
3. **Offer cocoa in committed batches.** Eight $3 preorders justify considering one small hot batch at the modeled costs. For sporadic individual orders, sealed milk is the simpler alternative. A scratch cocoa or special dispenser needs enough extra contribution or demand to justify taste testing and investment.
4. **Get the specific approval before fieldwork.** Once the site, seller, menu and process are known, resolve agency and host requirements. The evidence missing today is a written classification, a buyer at the quoted price, measured prep/route time, batch quality, actual costs, and a second full-price order. No venue booking, field sale, equipment purchase or public menu change formed part of this research.
