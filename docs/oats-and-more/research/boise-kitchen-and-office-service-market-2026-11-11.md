# Boise-area commercial kitchens and lower-capital office breakfast channels

**Live research date:** November 11, 2026. **Business:** Oats, Etc., oatmeal-first, no eggs in pilot. **Status:** research leads, not reservations or confirmed host/vendor invitations.

## Strategic conclusion

Instead of buying a specialty e-bike, trailer or shop, test the **licensed rented kitchen + 2017 Nissan Rogue + office delivery / portable breakfast station** model. A licensed commissary can prepare batches, but **does not automatically license direct retail service at an office**. Contact Central District Health (CDH) for the correct licensing route for (a) employer-paid catered breakfast, (b) individually preordered and collected bowls, and (c) walk-up individual sales from a temporary stand. Ask about cooking, safe transport, hot hold, handwashing/wastewater and site approvals.

## Four alternative selling models

1. **Employer-paid breakfast catering:** organization orders 20–50 bowls for employees or a team meeting; prep/pack in approved kitchen and deliver to host. **No retail trailer needed.** This is potentially easiest initial sales validation, but ask CDH about proper catering permits/food-handling and employer facility.
2. **Employee-paid office preorder and pickup:** building/HR emails a QR code; employees preorder by deadline; operator delivers in approved food-safe transport and distributes at approved private location. Can limit inventory to confirmed orders; direct on-site sales vs catered classification requires CDH and zoning clarification.
3. **Temporary indoor office breakfast counter:** rent licensed kitchen, transport oatmeal and toppings in Rogue, roll in a portable food-safe table/kiosk and approved holding/handwash setup; host supplies authorized outlet. Allows walk-up selling. Most similar to original 30–60 bowls / 3-hour goal, but has more licensing and on-site setup requirements.
4. **Counter residency/revenue-share** at an existing licensed café, coworking property, or venue. Possible path for trial mornings without owning a retail store; **no currently available lease/partnership confirmed** and must check whether the host's license permits independent operators.

Kitchen-only “ghost kitchen” monthly rent is unlikely to fit a 1–3 mornings per week experiment; recurring kitchen hours and client preorder commitments are preferable.

## Regulatory distinction to establish before promising office service

Central District Health: https://cdh.idaho.gov/licenses-permits-inspections/food-establishments/

CDH states TCS foods are regulated; licensing applies to restaurants, mobile trucks/carts, temporary and **catered events**. A **temporary food establishment** is normally a single festival/event operation **up to 14 consecutive days**, not an automatic blanket approval for a recurring daily office stand. A licensed prep kitchen alone doesn't provide a general retail-vending license.

**Ask CDH explicitly**: “I want to cook oatmeal at an approved shared commercial kitchen and transport it in my personal SUV, then either deliver 20–50 prepaid bowls to one company, distribute employee preorders at a private office building, or run a plug-in 3-hour breakfast table selling directly. What three classifications/permits, hot-transport requirements, handwash and food-protection systems would each model require?”

## Recommended next 5 real-world steps

1. Call **Osage** and **Gina's**, then **The Commons** or **Flava**, and request early prep availability, minimum hours and total fees.
2. Call **CDH** with exactly the three sale models above. Request classification of each **before buying equipment**.
3. Ask one office manager/HR/property manager whether they would promote an experimental **one-morning breakfast drop-off or sponsored employee breakfast**. Don't assume access just because it's private property.
4. Offer a **specific preorder menu** with a minimum viable quantity, fixed pickup/delivery window and all approved handling.
5. Use the pilot's paid orders, repeat rate, per-bowl costs and serving time to justify a more public recurring counter or trailer.

**Bottom line:** For Oats, Etc., the initial asset is the **office client relationship**, not a bicycle or trailer. The Rogue is suitable for investigating compact, properly secured and temperature-controlled transport if CDH approves the plan. Do not acquire a food trailer just to prove you can sell oatmeal.
