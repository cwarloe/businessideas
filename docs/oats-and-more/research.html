The direction

Three ways to test sales with far less equipment

Instead of owning a traveling storefront, start by securing a paying customer or willing host. All three models require confirming the relevant food-service and site approvals first.

01

Sponsored office breakfast

A business orders 20–50 portions for employees or a team meeting. Food is prepared and packaged by an appropriately licensed operation, then delivered.

Best first test of paid demand
02

Employee preorder pickup

Employees order and pay before a cutoff. Bring only committed quantities to an agreed pickup area and verify how CDH classifies distribution.

Best way to limit leftovers
03

Plug-in morning breakfast counter

Bring an approved portable warm-and-serve station to a cooperating office location for a 2–3 hour window. People order directly.

Best longer-term recurring format

Recommendation: don't buy mobility before you buy proof.

Find one office that wants to host a test breakfast; get the regulatory pathway confirmed; then make 30–50 real paid bowls. A distinctive sage-green counter can come later, after you've established recurring demand and know what the site requires.

A practical host pitch

Sell the convenience, not the equipment

“Oats, Etc. brings a warm, comforting breakfast to your workplace. Employees choose from a few oatmeal favorites, order ahead, and pick up breakfast without leaving the office. We handle preparation and service through an approved food operation.”

  • What an office manager gains: a low-effort employee amenity without managing a full café.
  • What employees gain: familiar oatmeal, thoughtful toppings, a predictable pickup window, and no detour on the way to work.
  • What Oats, Etc. gains: known demand, fewer leftovers, a recurring relationship and measurable willingness to pay.

Who might host a pilot?

Approach building managers and tenant-experience teams at multi-tenant office complexes. Existing coworking sites can also be interview prospects, but their on-site amenities and access rules may make them less attractive for paid breakfast vending. No researched office location has been confirmed to be accepting a recurring breakfast vendor.

Before soliciting paid orders

Three regulatory questions to settle

Central District Health says businesses handling time/temperature controlled foods may need a license and preoperational review, including catered events and mobile units. Renting a licensed kitchen is not automatically permission to operate a retail stand in an office. CDH official food-establishment guidance ↗

A

Employer pays

What permits and licensed-kitchen agreements cover an employer-sponsored breakfast delivered to one office?

B

Employees preorder

What rules cover transporting hot oatmeal for individually prepaid employee pickup at a private building?

C

Walk-up sales

What classification, site permission, handwash, approved holding and local zoning apply to a three-hour plug-in counter?

CDH generally defines a temporary food establishment as a single event lasting no more than 14 consecutive days; do not assume this automatically covers repeated weekday office sales. Hot holding, transport, prep, food protection and allergen management must be validated before the first service.

Recommended next actions

Five steps before purchasing a cart

Call two kitchens

Ask Osage and Gina's for early-morning prep availability, minimum hours, onboarding requirements, total one-session cost and permission to prepare food for office delivery.

Confirm the regulatory path with CDH

Describe employer-paid catering, individually prepaid pickup and walk-up counter service as three separate models, and ask for written guidance.

Identify a single host with concentrated morning demand

Contact an office/building manager. Ask about staff count, a breakfast benefit for tenants, a dedicated pickup space and internal promotion—not just power access.

Offer three simple breakfast recipes

Classic Brown Sugar & Cinnamon, Maple Walnut and Apple Pie Oats. Keep eggs and additional hot stations out of the first test.

Run a paid and permitted validation morning

Track committed orders, sold bowls, setup time, food temperature, service bottlenecks, leftovers, topping preferences and repeat interest. Reconsider a kiosk or trailer only after evidence.
