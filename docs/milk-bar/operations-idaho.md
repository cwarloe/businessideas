# Milk Bar — Idaho operating and financial learning guide

**Checked:** November 18, 2026 · **Purpose:** practical founder learning, not legal/tax advice, approval, or an operating forecast.

## Start with the business system

A drink counter has five flows: **customers** (discover → order → pay → pickup/flight), **ingredients** (source → receive → refrigerate → prepare → discard), **money** (sale → tax liability → variable costs → fixed costs → owner compensation), **people** (scheduling → safe prep → service → cleanup), and **compliance** (registration → approval → records → renewal). Design a simple daily log around all five.

### Daily close example (blank fields; no fabricated numbers)

| Field | Capture |
|---|---|
| Customers exposed / approached | Actual count and method |
| Orders / units by SKU | POS tally |
| Net sales excluding collected sales tax | POS report |
| Sales tax collected | Separate liability, **not revenue** |
| Milk opened / used / discarded by base | Volume and purchase cost |
| Ingredients, cups, lids, fees and other variable cost | Receipts and actual usage |
| Labor hours including owner sourcing, prep, setup, service, cleaning, admin | Time log |
| Site fee, transport, refrigeration and other period costs | Actual invoice |
| Complaints, allergies, queue delays, repeat purchases | Operational notes |

**Formula:** contribution per SKU = selling price before sales tax − milk/flavor/packaging/payment/attributable waste cost. Period cash result = total contribution − fixed costs − paid labor. Owner-time-adjusted result = cash result − (owner hours × chosen owner-hour value). Cash result is not necessarily accounting profit or taxable income.

**Illustrative arithmetic, not a prediction:** if a drink sold for $6.00 before tax and its variable cost were $2.00, contribution would be $4.00. At $400 of period fixed cost, 100 such drinks would cover fixed costs **before** owner labor, taxes on income, depreciation and other omitted costs. Replace every number with real quotes/receipts before making a decision.

## What to verify before serving anything

1. **Business registration and name:** The Idaho Secretary of State provides registration, assumed-name and trademark resources. Choose legal structure, search the proposed name and determine filings. Brand placeholders in the mockups are **not cleared**. [Idaho Secretary of State](https://sos.idaho.gov/business-resources/) (accessed 2026-11-18).
2. **Food establishment approval:** Central District Health (CDH) says food establishments offering time/temperature-controlled foods must be licensed and regulated, including permanent, mobile, temporary and catered formats. The application, plan review and pre-opening inspection are part of the process. CDH says permanent-establishment applications are due at least 30 days before planned opening; construction/remodel may take longer. Discuss each proposed format and the specific milk/flight menu **before** spending on a site or equipment. [CDH food establishments](https://cdh.idaho.gov/licenses-permits-inspections/food-establishments/) (accessed 2026-11-18).
3. **Sales tax and seller's permit:** Idaho's statewide sales tax rate is **6%** according to the Tax Commission. Its food/drinks guide states served food and drinks are taxable and sales tax must be separately shown on receipts; track remittance separately from sales. Confirm registration and treatment of the actual business format. [Tax basics](https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/sales-use/online-guide/), [Food and drink sales price](https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/sales-use/guides-for-certain-groups/food/food-drinks/sales-price/) (accessed 2026-11-18).
4. **Taxable purchases vs resale:** The Tax Commission distinguishes eligible ingredients and single-use customer containers from cleaning supplies, furniture and equipment. Check exemption documentation with an accountant. [Buying goods for resale](https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/sales-use/guides-for-certain-groups/food/food-drinks/buying-goods/) (accessed 2026-11-18).
5. **Location:** Confirm landlord/host authorization, city zoning and any local permits, signage, parking, water, wastewater, power and accessibility requirements. Requirements are **site-specific and not verified here**.
6. **Food safety:** Confirm cold holding, transport, date marking, handwashing and warewashing facilities, food-contact surfaces, allergen communication (milk, soy; possible nuts), cross-contact controls, approved suppliers and any food-handler/person-in-charge requirements directly with CDH. Do not assume a shared kitchen or homemade-food exemption covers prepared milk drinks.
7. **Insurance and employment:** Obtain appropriate quotes for general/product liability and property; if hiring, verify payroll, withholding, unemployment and workers' compensation obligations. No coverage or rates have been priced.

## Four launch models to compare

| Format | Why test it | Critical unknown |
|---|---|---|
| Approved hosted pop-up | Lower fixed commitment | Health approval, host permission, setup/cleanup and repeat measurement |
| Partner counter in licensed operation | Shared foot traffic/facilities may help | Whether existing license covers this operator/menu, rent/revenue share |
| Dedicated small counter | Brand control and repeat access | Rent, occupancy, foot traffic, equipment, staffing |
| Catered tasting event | Potential group transaction | Off-site transport/serving approval, travel and labor |

**No recommendation to open** until the least expensive compliant paid pilot shows adequate contribution **after owner time**. Track both appeals with the same hours, audience exposure, pricing and menu where possible.

## Immediate owner learning sequence

- Build a one-page startup cash list: deposits, permits, insurance, equipment, refrigeration, smallwares, initial inventory, working capital, contingency, recoverable resale value.
- Get two supplier quotes per proposed milk base and record package size, usable ounces, opened-product shelf life and spoilage.
- Make a recipe card for each test drink with ounces, prep seconds, vessel count, cleaning steps and allergy controls.
- Obtain written CDH classification for the specific service format and proposed menu.
- Use a pilot decision rule based on **paid orders, repeat purchase, contribution, waste and owner hours** rather than survey enthusiasm.

## Source and uncertainty register

Primary government sources checked 2026-11-18: Idaho SOS, Idaho Tax Commission, CDH (links above). **Verified published guidance** is not the same as a permit or site-specific ruling. **Unknown:** actual product cost, proposed lease/host terms, business name availability, zoning, inspection outcome, demand, customer frequency and profitability. Recheck sources and get professional advice before spending or serving.
