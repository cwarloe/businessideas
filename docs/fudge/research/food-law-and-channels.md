# Food-law and channel checks

**Researched November 15, 2026.** Use the current Idaho guidance rather than copying older oats or cottage-food notes.

Idaho DHW says the **Idaho Direct-to-Consumer Commerce Act became effective March 20, 2026 and superseded the former cottage-food rule**. Its current guidance requires disclosure of the uninspected/unlicensed status and potential allergens, producer name/contact information, and ingredients for multi-ingredient products. It says wholesale is not exempt under that Act and flags additional requirements for out-of-state sales. See [DHW’s current guidance](https://healthandwelfare.idaho.gov/health-wellness/community-health/food-safety), which links the statute. [Central District Health](https://cdh.idaho.gov/licenses-permits-inspections/food-establishments/) also directs home-food sellers to the updated rules.

This project does not assume every recipe or channel qualifies. Obtain answers for the exact operation before sales.

| Proposed arrangement | Question to resolve |
|---|---|
| Home production, direct preorder and Idaho pickup | Does the recipe and transaction fit the Act, and what exact notice/label should accompany it? |
| Employer buys gifts for staff/clients | How does this purchaser/recipient arrangement fit the direct-to-consumer definition? |
| Producer operates a table at a cafe or office | Who is the seller, and what do CDH, property owner and venue require for sales and samples? |
| Cafe purchases stock to resell | What licensed production and wholesale requirements apply? Do not assume direct-sale exemption carries over |
| Market booth | Does the organizer accept the product and kitchen arrangement, and what are the sampling, insurance and vendor conditions? |
| Mail orders | Keep interstate sales out of the first pilot while applicable requirements are resolved |

DHW’s linked statute is the authoritative starting point. A venue can have its own acceptance requirements. Do not promise unrefrigerated storage duration before evaluating the actual recipe and packaging.

## Tax and business setup

The [Idaho Tax Commission food-sales guide](https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/sales-use/guides-for-certain-groups/food/food-drinks/sales-price/) says food sales are taxable. The [small-seller exemption page](https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/sales-use/exemptions/sales-and-use-taxes-exemptions-for-individuals/small-seller-exemption/) explains a potential exception with specific conditions; do not assume eligibility. Confirm seller-permit and tax treatment for the intended operation. A food-law exemption is not automatically a tax exemption.

Also resolve business-name registration, home/property restrictions, product liability coverage, venue permission and any applicable city requirements for the actual setup. These are unanswered setup questions, not statements that every item requires a permit.
