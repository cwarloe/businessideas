### Preliminary federal trademark screen

**Exact scope of finding:** No matching federal application or registration was found for these full-name combinations in the official USPTO search on November 15, 2026. This is not a guarantee that they are unregistered everywhere, available to use, or registrable.

Official combined-mark query, all classes and without restricting status:

```
CM:((fudge AND grin*) OR (fudge AND huddl*) OR (fudge AND rambl*))
```

**Observed:** “No results found.” The component-word search checks more than exact phrase order and includes variants beginning with the displayed stems. It includes combinations with Grins, Huddle and Ramble. It does not comprehensively search similar sounds or meanings.

A focused related-food query was also reviewed:

Five results were displayed, all reviewed:

**Implication:** Fudge Ramble deserves closer review against RAMBLE COFFEE before selection; a different full name is not automatically a safe name. The other results also require comparison of the full marks and goods, rather than simply comparing class numbers.

## Keep three decisions separate

1. **Name:** What the business is called.
2. **Look:** Green gift-box, coral/teal sampler, or rust/forest mercantile.
3. **Reason to buy:** The particular occasion and offer in the campaign.

A name can remain consistent while campaigns change by occasion. Choose one main reason to buy on each sample homepage. Occasion-specific pages or advertisements can test the others.

## Design P — green gift-box / current The Fudge Parcel

Preserve the pine green, warm white, paper band and personal note. The visual promise is thoughtful and ready to give.

| Angle | Buyer and trigger | Headline | Offer and marketing execution |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1: A specific thank-you | Someone wants to thank a neighbor, coworker or friend who helped | They showed up for you. Send a little thanks. | One gift-ready box with a short note. Posts show familiar reasons to thank someone, then the finished gift and next pickup window |
| P2: The host gift | Guest needs something to bring tonight or this weekend | Invited over? Bring the good part. | A ready-to-give box. Lead with the actual pickup deadline and package size; show it arriving at a dinner table. Do not promise same-day pickup until supported |
| P3: Team appreciation | Manager wants to recognize several people without assembling gifts | Ten thank-yous. One easy order. | A proposed ten-box order with named notes and one handoff. Test with a buyer interview and then a real paid order; classification and packing capacity must be resolved first |

**First test:** P1 against P2 at the same weight and price. Measure completed purchases, occasion and packing/handoff time. P3 is a separate order-size test, not evidence the retail price works at wholesale.

## Design S — coral/teal sampler / current Fudge Social

Preserve the bold friendly type, numbered flavors and visible variety. The visual promise is discovery and interaction.

| Angle | Buyer and trigger | Headline | Offer and marketing execution |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1: Pick your favorite | Customer wants to try several flavors without choosing one large slab | Four flavors. Pick a side. | An 8 oz flight with four 2 oz portions and a tasting card. A carousel introduces each flavor, followed by a specific box offer; favorite votes support learning but do not count as demand |
| S2: Game-night contribution | Guest wants an easy treat that adds something to a gathering | Game night just got a fifth player. | A shareable sampler with a simple taste-and-rank card. Show friends comparing flavors; keep optional activities short and avoid turning the purchase into homework |
| S3: The personal treat | Buyer wants a small enjoyable break, without needing a gift occasion | Your afternoon could use better squares. | A small two-flavor format or individual portion. Test a permitted host pop-up or local preorder. Keep clear size/price choices; measure whether smaller tickets justify the selling time |

**First test:** S1 versus S2 using the same sampler; change the occasion and message, not the recipe and price simultaneously. Test S3 later because it changes format and economics.

## Design O — rust/forest mercantile / current Fudge Outpost

Preserve the warm rust, dark green, foothill illustration and sturdy take-home box. The visual promise is an inviting local stop; it does not imply Idaho-grown ingredients.

| Angle | Buyer and trigger | Headline | Offer and marketing execution |
|---|---|---|---|
| O1: Weekend ritual | Local customer wants a familiar Friday/Saturday treat | Your weekend starts with a sweet stop. | A predictable weekly pickup window and limited menu. Use a recurring weekly post with actual availability; measure repeat purchases outside holiday periods |
| O2: Bring something from Boise | Visitor or local wants a place-linked gift | Bring home a little Boise. | A local-gift box with an honestly worded origin card. Test with actual buyers before approaching retail stockists; only claim Boise-made once production location is established |
| O3: The after-adventure treat | Local group is heading home after a walk, outing or day together | The outing’s over. The good part isn’t. | A take-home sharing box offered through an approved host or scheduled pickup. Show the treat at the destination, not sitting in a hot car; make no performance or health claims |

**First test:** O1 before O2. A local weekly ritual can be tested without relying on visitor traffic or retailer distribution. O3 is an occasion to test, not an assumption that fudge is suitable for every outdoor storage condition.

## Testing and website guardrails

- Price and package weight must be visible. Proposed prices, pickup promises and availability stay labeled as examples until operationally supported.
- One primary call to action per homepage: choose a flight, choose a gift box, or view this week’s proposed box.
- Do not claim an operating shop, scheduled tasting, long family history, local ingredient sourcing or customer reviews without evidence.
- Keep owner time, packaging and delivery in the economics. Ten gift boxes can sell well and still be a poor use of time if fulfillment expands.
- Use sales and contribution per owner hour as decision evidence, with taste feedback and favorite votes as supporting information.
- Existing oats voting and infrastructure remain separate. No websites, checkout, polls or outreach were launched in this round.
