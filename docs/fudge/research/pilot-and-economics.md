# Pilot economics and operations

## Worked example — assumptions only

This is a planning model, not a supplier quote or forecast. All prices exclude sales tax. Replace every cost with measured batch records and actual receipts. No owner wage is included in cash contribution, so owner time is shown separately.

Assume **10 lb finished saleable fudge**, after trimming and samples, divided into **20 × 8 oz boxes**. Produce enough gross weight to cover documented losses; do not treat samples as free additional yield.

| Item | Assumption | Batch amount |
|---|---|---:|
| Ingredients, including allowance for losses/samples | $4 per saleable lb | $40 |
| Boxes, liners and labels | $1.50 per box | $30 |
| Card processing | 3% of sales + $0.30 per box transaction | $15.60 at $320 sales |
| Local transport and consumables | Batch allocation | $15 |
| Gross revenue | 20 boxes × $16 | $320 |
| Cash contribution before other overhead and tax | Revenue minus the four costs above | **$219.40** |
| Total owner time | Buying, cooking, cleaning, packing, selling, admin and handoff | 8 hours |
| Contribution per owner hour | $219.40 / 8 | **$27.43/hour** |
| Owner-time target for comparison | Illustrative $30/hour × 8 | $240 |
| Remainder after valuing that time | $219.40 − $240 | **−$20.60**, before other overhead |

This does not include kitchen rent, insurance, equipment, permits, advertising, refunds or income/self-employment taxes. It is not net profit or take-home pay. Card fees are hypothetical, not a quote from a selected processor.

## Sensitivity: sell all 20 boxes

Fixed batch costs here are $40 ingredients + $30 packaging + $15 transport = $85. With 20 separate card payments:

`Contribution = 20 × price × 0.97 − (20 × 0.30) − 85`

| Box price | Revenue | Cash contribution | Per owner hour at 8 hours |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $14 | $280 | $180.60 | $22.58 |
| $16 | $320 | $219.40 | $27.43 |
| $18 | $360 | $258.20 | $32.28 |

At these assumptions, covering $240 of owner time requires about **$17.07/box**, before the omitted overhead. At $16, it takes 6 sold boxes to recover the $85 batch cash spend after processing, but that does not recover labor. If only 15 of 20 sell, contribution drops to **$143.30**, or **$17.91/hour** at eight hours; treat remaining boxes as unsold inventory, not assumed future revenue.

An assumed $60 booth fee plus four extra owner hours reduces the $16 sellout example to **$159.40 / 12 = $13.28/hour**. This is why preorders deserve the first test. A $60 fee is a scenario, not a quoted Boise market fee.

## Keep the first operation simple

- One batch day and one handoff window; preorders before production where lawful and practical.
- Existing suitable kitchen equipment first: accurate scale, thermometer appropriate to the recipe, pans, cutting tools, sanitary storage and food-contact packaging. Price any missing items before setting a pilot budget.
- Record both active work and elapsed setting/cooling time. The latter affects scheduling and space even when it is not hands-on labor.
- Count saleable ounces, samples, trim, rejects and unsold units. Use actual yield to calculate cost per ounce.
- Confirm storage conditions and label each batch. “Fudge” alone does not establish shelf stability; heat can also impair quality during transport or display.
- Put a limit on custom flavors, delivery stops and personalized packaging until their time costs are known.

## Compare with Oats, Etc.

| Factor | Fudge hypothesis | Oats pilot hypothesis |
|---|---|---|
| Customer trigger | Treat, gift, celebration | Immediate breakfast need |
| Production timing | Batch ahead if recipe/storage supports it | Prep and hot-service schedule |
| First channel | Preorder and consolidated handoff | Concentrated morning host location |
| Repeat demand | Must prove occasions beyond holidays | Must prove repeated breakfast purchases |
| Main hidden cost | Packing, customer acquisition and delivery | Prep, setup, service window and cleanup |

Fudge may offer more scheduling flexibility. It is not automatically a better business: a premium small batch can still pay poorly after all owner hours are counted.
