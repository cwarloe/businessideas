# Consultant review of the business idea playbook

November 18, 2026. Independent AI review using the perspective of an experienced small-business launch and marketing consultant, followed by a primary-source curriculum comparison. Reviewed the playbook and templates at PR #18, Milk Bar market/feasibility reports in PRs #15–16, and the Oats office model in PR #17. This is a process assessment. Customer discovery and field results remain outstanding.

## Verdict and order

Keep the current emphasis on inexpensive learning, operator time, negative findings, and evidence gates. Improve the order by running customer conversations alongside an early check of legal, channel and economic constraints. A quick competitor screen helps frame the questions. Conversations about recent purchases should then help select a first buyer segment and offer. Refine pricing, acquisition, operations and cash needs together before a small paid pilot. Observe reorder behavior and update the working plan before increasing commitments.

The existing Milk Bar matched-headline test already records traffic, paid conversion, operational effects and repeat opportunity carefully. Its role is to refine a defined offer at a counter. Choosing between a cup, take-home bottle and delivery route requires a prior decision about buying occasion and distribution.

## What business and marketing classes would add

Course requirements differ. The comparisons below use published examples. They suggest extensions when the audience needs a formal plan or an assessed project.

MIT's 2013 New Enterprises assignments develop target customer/value proposition, market definition, competitive advantage, customer acquisition, sales/marketing/pricing, product, finance and human resources, followed by a revised executive summary and final paper. The recurring revisions are a useful model for keeping one consistent business plan. Its venture context differs from a small owner-operated business. [MIT assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-390-new-enterprises-spring-2013/pages/assignments/)

MIT's 2010 Marketing Management syllabus connects an analysis of company, customers, competitors, collaborators and context with segmentation and positioning, then decisions about product, price, promotion and place. It also assesses the ability to defend recommendations with qualitative and quantitative reasoning. Add a short explanation of why the chosen segment and marketing mix fit together, supported by actual evidence. [MIT marketing syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-810-marketing-management-fall-2010/pages/syllabus/)

Georgia Tech's published MGT3300 draft has market and consumer intelligence reports and a marketing presentation. Its stated outcomes include evaluating primary and secondary research. This supports documenting how data was collected and presenting a defensible recommendation. The PDF is a published draft with mixed date cues. It is an example of an assignment structure. [Georgia Tech published syllabus](https://syllabus.gatech.edu/sites/default/files/2026-03/MGT%203300%20Syllabus_accessible_2026%20draft.pdf)

SBA supports both short lean plans and detailed traditional plans. Traditional coverage includes management, marketing/sales, funding and financial projections. Its detailed forecast guidance includes cash flow, income statements, balance sheets and capital budgets, with greater first-year detail. For a larger commitment, expand our shift model into a monthly operating and cash forecast and identify who runs the business. Match the forecast horizon and detail to the recipient. [SBA business plan guidance](https://legacy.sba.gov/business-guide/plan-your-business/write-your-business-plan)

### Suggested assignment packet

These deliverables are our adaptation of the sources above.

1. A short executive recommendation stating the customer, offer, investment sought and supporting evidence.
2. A market assessment with an initial segment, reachable market estimate, substitutes and competitor comparison. Explain the assumptions connecting the reachable market to sales.
3. A discovery appendix with recruitment, questions, anonymized observations, conflicting findings and limitations.
4. A marketing plan with positioning, a coordinated product/price/place/promotion decision, acquisition budget, sales process and retention measures.
5. An operations and team plan with responsible people, skills gaps, suppliers, dependencies and capacity.
6. A financial model with acquisition costs, contribution, overhead, labor, startup funding, monthly cash flow and downside. Add formal statements and longer forecasts where the assignment or funding audience calls for them.
7. A test and revision record showing a prediction, observed outcome and the resulting plan change.
8. A brief presentation and response to skeptical questions. Ask a reviewer to trace one sales forecast back through audience, channel, conversion, price, capacity and costs.

## Implications for the two ideas

**Oats, Etc.** Begin with a specific office buyer and an actual recent breakfast order. An employer delivery is a concrete first offer, but the published model leaves little cushion at its assumed hours. Obtain a complete kitchen quote, price the full service, and measure the whole route. Keep a bowl-only baseline. Cocoa remains an optional batch whose added margin must justify its work. [Office assessment](https://github.com/cwarloe/businessideas/pull/17)

**Milk Bar.** Preserve the owner's flexibility about cups, warm milk, take-home bottles and delivery. Evaluate each against a specific buyer's current habit and an existing substitute. Model supplier-sealed bottle resale and route density separately from the cup/flight model before comparing returns. A cold-counter inquiry covers a narrower process than hot service or packaged delivery. ## Limits of the review

The consultant is an AI agent using the documented material and a requested professional perspective. Its agreement with the previous analysis adds critique and consistency checking. Actual buyer behavior, quotes, operational timing and authority determinations supply the next evidence. Neither idea has demonstrated repeatable profit in the reviewed records. The recommendations do not establish a universal academic grading rubric.
