# Idaho example · starting and running the business

Researched November 18, 2026. Idaho is the default jurisdiction for the current Oats and Milk Bar examples. Boise/Ada County is a location scenario. Apply the same method with the actual city, county, health district, entity and sales model when they are selected.

Use this with the [beginner financial guide](running-the-business.md) and [Idaho obligations register](idaho-obligations-template.md). Learning and assessment can happen before registration. The rows below show what to resolve when preparing to trade, hire, take payments or acquire assets. No entity, bank account, permit or tax account has been created through this research.

## The business has several separate responsibilities

| Responsibility | What it means in Idaho | When to resolve / evidence to keep |
|---|---|---|
| Business identity and structure | Idaho registers names/entities through the Secretary of State. State guidance describes an exception for a sole proprietor trading using the qualifying full personal name. A DBA alone does not create an LLC or liability protection. | Choose ownership and legal/tax setup before operating under a business identity. Retain actual registration or the basis for an exception. [I01] [I02] |
| State and local permissions | Idaho has no general state business license. Industry requirements and local licensing, zoning, home-occupation or property permissions may still apply. | Use the state Business Wizard and the actual local authority. Record the precise activity/address and response. [I01] [I03] |
| Federal identifier and tax setup | Check the IRS rules for whether the entity or employment setup needs an EIN and which federal returns/payments apply. | Verify the chosen setup before registration/payroll steps that depend on it. Keep identifiers private. [I04] |
| Idaho tax accounts | Idaho Business Registration establishes applicable sales/use and employee-related accounts. | Determine which accounts apply to the actual business and complete them at the appropriate stage. Keep notices and assigned filing schedules. [I05] |
| Retail goods, food and drinks | Idaho's state sales/use tax rate is 6%. Food/drink sales are generally taxable, subject to the actual exemption and transaction treatment. | Confirm taxable items, applicable exemptions, local additions and invoice treatment before charging tax. [I06] [I07] |
| Small-seller possibility | A limited exemption may apply to qualifying Idaho individuals/sole proprietors. Entity, location, purchases and sales conditions matter. | Verify eligibility with the Tax Commission before building it into the model. Track changing eligibility. No exemption is assumed for Oats or Milk Bar. [I08] |
| Sales/use filing and cash | A regular seller permit has an assigned filing cycle, generally including zero-sales returns. The Commission says sales tax follows the sale period even if an invoiced customer has not paid. | Calendar the assigned due date and reconcile taxable sales, refunds, collection and liability. A monthly filer generally pays/files by the following month's 20th, adjusted for weekends/holidays. [I09] |
| Income and self-employment taxes | The entity and owner's situation affect federal and Idaho obligations. Federal estimates may apply. Idaho guidance says individuals have no required state estimated payments, while allowing voluntary payments. | Get the correct entity/owner treatment and payment plan. Keep business profit, owner withdrawals and sales-tax funds visible. [I10] [I11] |
| Employment and helpers | A paid operator may create payroll, withholding, unemployment, workers' compensation and reporting duties. The relationship needs classification. | Resolve the relationship before work begins. Price employer costs and administration. [I12] [I13] [I14] |
| Food operation | CDH regulates relevant food operations in Ada County. Prepared oatmeal, poured cold milk, warmed milk and supplier-sealed resale can involve different processes. | Identify the seller, operator, product, prep/storage/transport/service and site. Obtain the determination and approvals applicable to the selected activity. [I15] |
| Ongoing entity records | Entities can have annual-report and registered-agent responsibilities. State guidance says annual reports have no filing charge and DBAs do not file them. | Confirm the actual entity record, renewal cycle, notices and contact/address updates through SOSBiz. [I01] [I02] |
| Equipment/property | Counties assess business personal property and apply exemptions. Tax Commission guidance describes equipment and exemptions, including a combined $250,000 per-county exemption under its conditions. | Keep equipment purchase/location records and ask the county assessor what reporting/exemption applies. Confirm first-year reporting even when a small operation expects an exemption. [I16] |
| Change, pause or close | A pause in sales does not automatically settle account duties. | Close/update tax accounts and registrations as appropriate, resolve outstanding bills and records, and verify continuing return requirements. [I05] [I09] |

## What sales tax looks like in the records

Original teaching illustration. Assume a taxable $10 food sale, a valid seller arrangement, 6% state tax, no additional applicable taxes, and a hypothetical $0.40 processor fee.

| Record | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Product sale revenue | $10.00 |
| Tax collected | $0.60 |
| Customer pays | $10.60 |
| Processor fee | −$0.40 |
| Bank receives | $10.20 |

The $10.20 deposit includes money owed for tax. Before food and other operating costs, revenue less the processing expense is $9.60. Record the $0.60 liability and its later payment to the state. Remitting it reduces cash and the liability. It does not create a second operating expense for the same collected amount. Confirm actual invoice, posting and tax treatment with the bookkeeper. [I06] [I07]

If a customer buys on an invoice and pays late, the sales-tax due date may arrive before collection. Model that timing in the cash forecast. A separately stated delivery charge and a food/service charge can have different treatment under the state's food guide. Have the selected invoice reviewed instead of assuming one treatment for every fee. [I07] [I09]

## If someone else operates it

Decide whether the person owns the business, is an employee, or has another properly classified arrangement. Record who buys stock, prices orders, receives sales, controls quality, pays bills, handles refunds and bears losses.

Idaho's Industrial Commission says employers generally need workers' compensation for one or more employees, including part-time/seasonal staff, unless specifically exempt, with coverage in place before the first employee is hired. Idaho generally requires reporting a new employee within 20 days of hire. Covered employers also have unemployment reporting/payment responsibilities. Verify classification and applicable exceptions with the responsible agencies. [I12] [I13] [I14]

## A practical calendar to learn

- **Every operating day** — Record sales, tax where applicable, fees, cash, stock/waste and hours. Preserve invoices/receipts and quality records.
- **Weekly** — Reconcile deposits and collections, check unpaid invoices and upcoming supplier/payroll/tax bills, and update cash availability.
- **At each assigned tax period** — Reconcile the applicable return to the records, file, pay and retain confirmation. Follow the agency-assigned frequency.
- **Monthly management review** — Close the books, inspect stock and amounts owed, read profit/cash/balance-sheet results, and decide on one operating improvement.
- **At annual or event-based triggers** — Check entity reports, insurance/permits, employment changes, equipment reporting, tax returns and account changes.

The routine is our practical adaptation. The actual legal calendar comes from the selected entity, agency notices, current rules and professional advice. Track each duty in the register, even when the answer is 'not applicable' with a reason. Keep filing confirmations and private account details outside the public repository.

## How this changes the two examples

**Oats.** A group invoice or separate bowl sales needs a clearly identified seller and invoice/tax process. A rented kitchen and office delivery add operator/site and food-process questions. A hired helper adds an employment branch. Build those costs and due dates into the operating model.

**Milk.** A cup, warmed product, supplier-sealed bottle and delivery route need separate sales and food-handling treatment. Determine the product's tax treatment, supplier/resale terms, and required operating permissions for the actual model. The previous cold-counter inquiry establishes no approval for a different process.

At this stage, use the register in PLANNING status. Selection of the operator, entity, actual location and sales model will determine the accounts, approvals and filing calendar.
