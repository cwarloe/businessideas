# Running a small business · beginner operating guide

Prepared November 18, 2026. This adds financial and operating literacy to the [business development framework](coverage-framework.md). The independent AI consultant recommended practicing one complete business cycle, then using a repeatable review. You can do the exercises before spending money, registering an entity or contacting customers.

**Learning goal** — Be able to explain what was sold, what was earned, what cash is available and committed, what the business owns/owes, and what action to take next.

## Four lessons

| Lesson | Work through | Demonstrate understanding |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Price the complete job | One offer's revenue, consumed materials, fees, waste, acquisition, delivery, overhead and all work hours | Calculate contribution, cash left, operator return and downside. Explain price changes if volume or hours differ. |
| 2. Record the transaction | Quote/order, delivery, invoice, receipt, payment, processor settlement and inventory use | Trace the records without counting an invoice and its collection as two sales. Identify owner money and missing documents. |
| 3. Read the financial picture | The profit and loss statement, balance sheet, cash flows and changes in equity | Explain why profit, cash, owner withdrawals and personal earnings can differ. |
| 4. Manage the next period | Forecast cash dates, compare actual results with the plan, identify the cause and choose one correction | Explain whether the next order can be funded and how price, stock, workload or collection terms should change. |

Read the [practice business month](practice-business-month.md), answer its questions, then check the linked statements. Use the [operating-review template](operating-review-template.md) to apply the lesson. A rehearsal can stay hypothetical until an actual transaction exists.

## Basic financial language

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| Revenue | Value of sales earned during the period under the accounting approach used |
| Cost of goods sold | Recorded cost of the products sold |
| Gross profit | Revenue less cost of goods sold |
| Operating expenses | Other costs of running the business during the period |
| Inventory | Usable goods or materials still held for future use or sale |
| Receivable | Money a customer owes |
| Payable | Money the business owes a supplier or other party |
| Asset | A resource the business owns or controls, such as cash, inventory or equipment |
| Liability | An obligation the business owes |
| Equity | The owner's residual interest, assets minus liabilities |

The profit and loss statement reports performance over a period. A balance sheet reports financial position on a date. Cash flows explain movements in cash. A statement of equity connects owner contributions, earnings and withdrawals. [Financial statements and their relationships](https://openstax.org/books/principles-financial-accounting/pages/2-1-describe-the-income-statement-statement-of-owners-equity-balance-sheet-and-statement-of-cash-flows-and-how-they-interrelate)

## Use the right number for a decision

- **Contribution** is revenue less the variable costs of making and fulfilling the order. It must cover fixed expenses and compensation for the work.
- **Accounting profit** reflects the accounting policies and costs recognized for the period. An unpaid owner can still have substantial time invested.
- **Return on operator time** divides the amount left for that operator by all their work hours. If wages are already deducted, explain whose time and remaining return this measures.
- **Economic surplus** subtracts a chosen value of unpaid owner work and applicable investment cost. Avoid charging wages, owner-time value or capital recovery twice.
- **Cash forecast** asks when receipts and payments occur. Funding inventory, receivables and upcoming obligations can require money before profit turns into cash.
- **Markup and margin** use different denominators. At a $5 cost and $8 selling price, the $3 difference is 60% of cost and 37.5% of selling price. Additional expenses reduce what remains.

The practice example gives exact reconciled arithmetic for these decisions. All examples are educational assumptions.

## Keep records that can be checked

Maintain identifiable business records and separate business and personal money when operating. Keep invoices, receipts, supplier bills, refunds, inventory counts, bank statements and payment settlements, linked by date and a unique reference. Reconcile the records with account statements and investigate differences. IRS Publication 583 explains recordkeeping, separate accounts and monthly reconciliation. [IRS Publication 583, December 2024 revision](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p583)

A useful transaction log records date, reference, counterparty code, purpose/category, earned sale or incurred cost, tax where applicable, cash received/paid, payment method and balances still outstanding. Track owner funding, borrowing, customer advances, transfers and withdrawals distinctly. Keep business obligations and the customer's advance payment visible in the cash forecast. Have an accountant confirm actual recognition, entity and tax treatment before relying on it.

For a card sale, reconcile the order's sale amount, tax collected where applicable, refunds, fees, pending settlement and net deposit. A deposit can combine several transactions. A refund or timing difference needs explanation. Verify the actual processor statement and the bookkeeper's posting method.

## A manageable operating routine

These are recommended management habits. Use them when transactions begin and adapt the timing to the business.

| When | Review | Decision/output |
|---|---|---|
| Each operating day | Orders and delivery promises, actual sales/collections/refunds, receipts, stock/waste, quality issues and all work hours | Complete records, known customer follow-ups, safe next stock/service plan and reconciled cash/POS totals |
| Weekly | Settlements, unpaid invoices/bills, near-term cash forecast, contribution, capacity and next orders | Decide what can be funded, what to collect/pay/order, and whether price or workload needs changing |
| Monthly | Reconcile bank and processor accounts, count inventory, review receivables/payables and equipment, prepare financial statements | Compare actual with budget, explain variances, choose one correction and update the working plan |
| Before a major commitment | Cash after all obligations, downside, operator capacity and the return expected from the investment | Proceed, revise or defer with a spending limit and evidence |
| At a required reporting date | Verified tax, payroll, license, insurance and contract obligations | Submit/pay/renew as required for the chosen entity, location and activity |

SBA's management guidance covers bookkeeping, financing, people, taxes, ongoing compliance, assets, marketing and risk. Use those topics to check ongoing responsibilities once the operation is defined. [SBA management guidance](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/manage-your-business/)

## Financial controls and operator handoff

Record who can quote a price, buy stock, accept an order, issue a refund, move money and approve an exception. Decide who checks sales against collections and supplier bills. When a helper operates the stand, give them clear portion, quality, stock, cash and refund instructions. Review the records regularly. Where practical, have another person check reconciliations and unusual transactions.

Record equipment purchases and useful life separately from supplies consumed. Borrowing, principal payments, interest, depreciation and taxes need the correct categories. Ask for bookkeeping/accounting help when those enter the model. Keep source documents and a list of questions ready so the professional can resolve specific issues. MIT's introductory accounting sequence includes transactions, accrual accounting, inventory, cash flows and long-term assets. [MIT financial and managerial accounting, Spring 2004](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-501-introduction-to-financial-and-managerial-accounting-spring-2004/pages/lecture-notes/)

For owner compensation, distinguish the management question of what the work should earn from the legal/accounting method of paying the owner. Wages, draws, distributions and taxes depend on the chosen setup. Identify the actual operator and check the treatment with qualified guidance. Record amounts earmarked for obligations and future operating cash before considering a withdrawal.

## Questions to bring to a bookkeeper or accountant

1. Which entity/accounting treatment fits the actual business and how should owner money be recorded?
2. Which taxes and payroll obligations apply, when are they due, and how should collected amounts be tracked?
3. How should inventory, waste, deposits, equipment and refunds be recorded?
4. How do we reconcile processor deposits and produce reliable monthly statements?
5. What ongoing service and review are useful at this transaction volume, and what would they cost?

The framework leaves entity selection, tax rates, filing dates and personal financial decisions open until the relevant facts and current guidance are available.

## Evidence of operating readiness

Before committing to serve, use a simulation to demonstrate a reconciled transaction record, a feasible cash calendar, and a complete process for orders, quality, stock and customer issues. The operating person should explain the practice example and one response to a shortfall. Resolve legal and safety requirements specific to any real activity.
