# One-day trial permitting checklist

**This is the headline deliverable for the kitchen and permitting scout.**  
**Date checked:** November 11, 2026 (America/Boise).  
**For:** Charles Warloe, Oats, Etc. (Modern Comfort Kitchen).  
**How to use this file:** Read the short answer, pick which trial you mean, then work the checklists and call the agencies yourself. Log answers in the tables at the end. All scripts are drafts for you. This scout did not call, email, or submit anything.

**Disclaimer:** Regulations change. Fees change. The agency's answer on your exact menu, site, and setup governs. Nothing in this file is a permit or a legal opinion.

## Short answer: does a one-day trial need the full license?

**It depends on where you sell and how the food is served. A one-day trial does not automatically need the $200 permanent food-establishment license, but it is also not automatically license-free.**

1. **Hot oatmeal served to people on the spot (a stand, table, cart, or office counter) is not covered by Idaho's new homemade-food law.** The Idaho Direct-to-Consumer Commerce Act, effective March 20, 2026, replaced the old cottage-food rule. It can allow some homemade shelf-stable and perishable foods sold direct to an informed end consumer in Idaho, with a required notice and records. It expressly does **not** include food that is "prepared, cooked, or served on-site for immediate consumption, including restaurant-style food service, mobile food units, concession trailers, or other facilities preparing ready-to-eat meals." Source: [Idaho Code 37-203(5)](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/title37/t37ch2/sect37-203/), and the [Idaho Department of Health and Welfare food safety page](https://healthandwelfare.idaho.gov/health-wellness/community-health/food-safety), both checked November 11, 2026.

2. **If the trial is a booth at a public event or celebration (festival, farmers market, street fair, parade), Central District Health uses a Temporary Food Establishment (TFE) license, not the full permanent license.** CDH defines a TFE as a food booth or stand that operates for no more than fourteen days in a row "in conjunction with a single event or celebration, such as a festival, farmers market, street fair, or parade." Published fees on November 11, 2026: **$35 for one day**, $45 for a single event of two or three days, and $80 for four days or multiple events. Submit the application **at least 30 days before the event**. CDH's temporary-event handout says the license is issued **at the event after a passing inspection**. That is still a license, with an approved menu and (for TCS food) approved prep. It is not the $200 permanent establishment license, and it is not a standing license to sell breakfast at an office every weekday. Sources: [CDH Food Establishments and Food Safety](https://cdh.idaho.gov/licenses-permits-inspections/food-establishments/) and [Temporary Food Establishment Information (PDF)](https://cdh.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Temporary-Food-Establishment-Information.pdf), checked November 11, 2026. Statutory fee amounts are also in [Idaho Code 39-1607](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/title39/t39ch16/sect39-1607/).

3. **Whether a private office one-morning paid trial counts as a "single event or celebration" under those TFE rules is UNVERIFIED.** CDH's definition lists festivals, markets, street fairs, and parades. A hosted office breakfast is not on that list. CDH also says events open to the general public are not considered catered events, and that a restaurant or caterer's annual license does not automatically cover a temporary booth at a public event. The reverse is also a risk: a TFE may not cover a private office service. **You must ask CDH to classify this exact fact pattern before you set a sale date.** How to confirm: use Script A below, and ask them to follow up in email if they will.

4. **A free tasting may be treated differently from a paid sale, but that is also UNVERIFIED for this menu.** CDH publishes "Guidelines for Sampling Demos" on its [food establishments page](https://cdh.idaho.gov/licenses-permits-inspections/food-establishments/). Ask CDH whether a free office tasting of hot oatmeal, with no money collected, still needs a TFE, a catering classification, or something else. Do not collect preorders or payment until CDH answers the payment question.

5. **Even a one-day TFE is not a "cook at home and show up" path for this product, unless CDH expressly says otherwise under the 2026 Act.** CDH's farmers-market handout (updated December 28, 2023, so it predates the Act) says potentially hazardous food "must be prepared in a facility that is approved by the FDA, USDA, Health Department, or be prepared on site" and "may not be prepared in a home kitchen." That same handout still lists a $72 multiple-day fee, which does not match the $80 figure on CDH's current webpage, so treat the 2023 handout as possibly stale and confirm with CDH. Source: [Farmers Market Information (PDF)](https://cdh.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Farmers-Market-Information.pdf), checked November 11, 2026.

6. **City of Boise, insurance, and ACHD are separate from the health license.** A TFE from CDH does not replace a City vendor, temporary-merchant, or food-and-beverage license if the City says you need one. It does not replace insurance. ACHD matters only if any part of the setup is in a public right-of-way (sidewalk, curb, planter strip). Details are in the checklists below.

**Bottom line to take to the phone:** Plan on a licensed or on-site prep path and a 30-day CDH lead time for any paid public-event trial. Do not assume a private office morning is either "just a TFE" or "just homemade food." Get CDH to say which box it is.

## Timeline (work backwards from the trial date)

CDH says to submit a TFE application at least **30 days** before the event. Permanent and mobile applications also say at least 30 days, and the mobile plan-review packet says to notify CDH at least **15 days** before opening for a pre-operational inspection. A "one-day trial" is not a last-minute project.

Suggested sequence you can run from a Windows PC or a phone:

1. **Now:** Call CDH (Script A). Get the classification and the application name. Ask whether a licensed kitchen is required for your exact menu and site.
2. **Same week:** If they say you need approved prep, call two or three kitchens (see [kitchens.md](kitchens.md) and [call-scripts.md](call-scripts.md)). Ask about early-morning hours and a commissary agreement.
3. **Same week:** Call City Clerk (Script B) and, if the site is a private lot, Planning (Script C). Call ACHD (Script D) only if placement might touch a sidewalk, curb, or planter.
4. **Same week:** Call your insurance agent (Script E) with whatever CDH, the City, the host, or the kitchen said they require.
5. **After CDH tells you the form:** Submit the CDH application in the Accela portal on a browser (phone or Windows). Do not mail a form until you know that is still accepted. Portal: [https://cdh.idaho.gov/accela/](https://cdh.idaho.gov/accela/).
6. **Keep the 30-day clock:** Do not advertise a paid date until the application is in and CDH has not objected to the date.

## What to have ready before you call or apply

Have these in front of you (a notes app on your phone is enough):

- Business name you would use that day (Oats, Etc. is not legally cleared in the research archive; say it is a working name).
- The exact menu: plain cooked oatmeal; toppings (brown sugar, cinnamon, raisins, maple, walnuts or pecans, apple topping if used); packaging (bowl, lid, spoon); drinks only if you will actually sell them.
- How it will be cooked, cooled or hot-held, and moved (licensed kitchen versus home, electric kettles, target hot-hold at 135°F or above, cooler plan if any).
- Date, start and end time (for example 6:30 to 9:30 a.m.), and full street address of the site, including city.
- Whether the public can walk up, or only employees of one company.
- Whether money will change hands that day (paid bowls, employer invoice, or free samples).
- Site sketch: table or kiosk, warmer locations, handwashing, trash, power cord route, and the line between private pavement and the sidewalk. Photos from your phone help.
- Written host permission if you have it, or the host's name and number.
- Your contact email so you can ask the agency to confirm in writing.

**Planning / zoning:** (208) 608-7100, zoninginfo@cityofboise.org, from the City's small-business and planning materials. Home occupation checklist: [Administrative Home Occupation Application](https://www.cityofboise.org/media/17933/202411-administrative-home-occupation-checklist.pdf).  
**Licensing:** [Vendor license](https://www.cityofboise.org/departments/city-clerk/licensing/other/vendor-license/), [Food and Beverage license](https://www.cityofboise.org/departments/city-clerk/licensing/food-and-alcohol-beverage/food-and-beverage-license/), [fee schedule](https://www.cityofboise.org/departments/city-clerk/licensing-fee-schedule/).  
**Code:** [Title 3, Chapter 5](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/boise_id/latest/boise/0-0-0-88558) (Ordinance 29-26, June 30, 2026); vendor rules in Title 3, Chapter 2, including the 2026 vendor amendments.

Published City fees on November 11, 2026 (plus a $3.00 license processing fee listed separately; "all fees are subject to change"):

- Eating and Drinking / Food and Beverage License: $84.00
- Vendor License, annual: $148.00
- Vendor License, semi-annual: $74.00
- Temporary Merchant License: $69.75

Vendor and temporary-merchant applications may also need a cash or surety bond of $1,000 per person or $5,000 per company. Temporary merchants and solicitors may need in-person fingerprinting at City Hall after the online application. All business license applications, including renewals, must include a current Idaho Secretary of State business registration that matches the name on the application. Source: the City pages linked above, checked November 11, 2026.

Boise City Code 3-5-6(B)(7) exempts "the sale of homemade shelf-stable or perishable food by the producer (or their designated agent) directly to the informed end consumer" under the Idaho Direct-to-Consumer Commerce Act from the **City Food and Beverage Establishment License**. That exemption helps only if CDH and the City agree your trial actually fits the Act. A hot walk-up breakfast stand is unlikely to fit. The exemption does not automatically cancel a vendor license, a zoning review, or a home-occupation review.

The City's definition of a mobile food and beverage establishment is broad. It includes trucks, trailers, pushcarts, bicycles, temporary sheds, and also "tables, hand-carried items, and portable containers" used for retail sale at temporary locations. Source: [Boise City Code 3-5-5](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/boise_id/latest/boise/0-0-0-88558), checked November 11, 2026. Ask the Clerk how they classify your exact setup.

If the trial is inside a Boise park or on the Greenbelt, call Parks and Recreation at (208) 608-7600 (number published on the vendor license page).

## 3. ACHD checklist (only if the right-of-way is in play)

Ada County Highway District manages much of the public roadway and sidewalk right-of-way in Ada County. **If the entire setup, the line, and the power cord stay on private pavement that the owner controls, ACHD is probably not your first call.** If any part sits on a sidewalk, curb, driveway apron, or landscaped strip that may be public right-of-way, it is relevant.

**How to get a number:** The City of Boise vendor page tells people with questions about public roads, sidewalks, or rights-of-way to contact ACHD at **(208) 387-6100**. Source: [Vendor License](https://www.cityofboise.org/departments/city-clerk/licensing/other/vendor-license/), checked November 11, 2026. ACHD's own site is [achdidaho.org](https://www.achdidaho.org/). Official ROW map aid: [ACHD Official ROW MapServer](https://gis.achdidaho.org/server/rest/services/ArcGIS_Hub/Official_ROW/MapServer). The map is a planning aid, not a substitute for ACHD's determination.

A more specific Development Services number appears in some City planning materials. Treat any number other than (208) 387-6100 as something to confirm on ACHD's site or by asking the main operator, because ACHD's contact page could not be fetched in this research session (the request was blocked).

Checklist:

- [ ] Looked at the site in person and compared the sidewalk and curb to the host's understanding of the property line.
- [ ] If there is any doubt, called ACHD (Script D) with the address and photos.
- [ ] Asked whether a permit is required for a temporary stand, a vehicle, or a cord in the right-of-way.
- [ ] Did not plan to run an extension cord across a public sidewalk. Boise vendor rules also address public-way utility connections.

## 4. Insurance checklist

No official state fee schedule sets your policy limits. Do not invent a premium. Ask your own insurance agent, and ask each kitchen, market, and host what they require in writing.

What is published:

- Capital City Public Market's vendor page requires **$1,000,000 general liability insurance**, plus an Idaho seller's permit (or temporary seller's permit or ST-124), a Temporary Food Establishment Permit, and a 10-by-10 white canopy with 20-pound weights on each leg. Applications were in a mixed "closed" / "open for the holiday season" state on the same page on November 11, 2026. Call the market office at (208) 345-3499 or ccpmarket@gmail.com to confirm. Source: [Becoming a Vendor](https://www.capitalcitypublicmarket.com/apply), checked November 11, 2026.
- The Commons kitchen's Kitchen Door listing requires "Proof of Insurance," plus ServSafe and a business license. It does not publish a dollar limit on that page. Source: [Kitchen Door, The Commons](https://www.thekitchendoor.com/kitchen-rental/the-commons), checked November 11, 2026.
- Other kitchens did not publish a limit on the pages checked this date. Ask them.

Ask your agent about:

- Commercial general liability (on-site injury, slip, spill).
- Product liability (foodborne illness and product claims). Confirm it covers the actual menu and sales method.
- Auto coverage if you transport food in the Rogue. A personal auto policy may not cover business use. **UNVERIFIED for your policy.** Ask the agent.
- Naming a kitchen, host, market, or city as additional insured, and any required certificate wording.
- The City's vendor bond ($1,000 / $5,000) if a vendor or temporary-merchant license applies. A bond is not the same thing as liability insurance.
- Whether a one-day event rider is cheaper than an annual policy for a single trial.

Checklist:

- [ ] Collected written insurance requirements from CDH (if any), the City, the host, the kitchen, and the event.
- [ ] Called your agent (Script E) with those requirements and the trial description.
- [ ] Logged limits, deductibles, additional-insured cost, and whether product liability is included. All figures come from the agent, not from this file.
- [ ] If you use a market or kitchen, got a certificate of insurance in the name they require, before the trial date.

## 5. Business license and tax questions (do not file yet)

This scout does not file anything. These are questions for you, and then for the agencies, when you are ready.

**Idaho Secretary of State** does not issue a food license. It registers entities and assumed business names. An assumed business name (ABN / DBA) is not a business license and does not create an entity. City of Boise license applications require a current SOS registration that matches the name on the application. SOS Business Services: (208) 334-2301, SOSBiz at [sos.idaho.gov](https://sos.idaho.gov/business-resources/business-faq/), Business Office 450 N. 4th Street, Boise, ID 83702, Monday through Friday 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m.

**Idaho State Tax Commission:** if you sell at retail in Idaho you likely need a seller's permit through the Idaho Business Registration process, after SOS registration. [Getting Tax Permits](https://tax.idaho.gov/online-services/business-registration/). Phone (208) 334-7660 or 1-800-972-7660.

**EIN:** issued by the IRS, not the State. Needed in some entity situations. [IRS EIN](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online).

Checklist (questions only):

- [ ] Asked SOS or a business advisor whether you should operate as a sole proprietor, LLC, or something else. This folder does not recommend a structure.
- [ ] Searched existing names in SOSBiz before you fall in love with a filing. "Oats, Etc." is still not cleared in the project research archive.
- [ ] Asked the City whether they need the SOS registration before they will accept a one-day vendor or food-and-beverage application.
