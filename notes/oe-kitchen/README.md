## Key findings (short)

- **A walk-up stand serving hot oatmeal for people to eat right away is not "homemade" food under the 2026 Idaho Direct-to-Consumer Commerce Act.** The Act replaced the old cottage-food rule on March 20, 2026, and it can cover some packaged perishable foods sold direct to an informed end consumer in Idaho. It expressly does **not** cover food prepared, cooked, or served on-site for immediate consumption, including restaurant-style service, mobile food units, and concession trailers. Source: [Idaho Code 37-203(5)](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/title37/t37ch2/sect37-203/), checked November 11, 2026.
- **For a one-day trial at a public event (festival, farmers market, street fair), CDH uses a Temporary Food Establishment license, not the $200 permanent establishment license.** Published one-day fee is $35. Apply at least 30 days before the event. The license is issued at the event after a passing inspection. That is still a license, just a different and cheaper one. It does not by itself authorize a recurring office stand. Sources: [CDH food establishments page](https://cdh.idaho.gov/licenses-permits-inspections/food-establishments/) and [Temporary Food Establishment Information](https://cdh.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Temporary-Food-Establishment-Information.pdf), checked November 11, 2026.
- **Whether a private office one-morning paid trial counts as a "single event or celebration" under those TFE rules is UNVERIFIED.** Ask Central District Health before promising a date. See the checklist.
- **Best published kitchen leads for a small, early-morning pilot** (advertised rates only, confirm before booking): Gina's Kitchen in Boise at $40/hour with storage included and 24/7 listed access; Osage Kitchen in Garden City at $40/hour; Flava Kitchen in Boise at $30/hour (possible session length to confirm). The Commons on Federal Way lists detailed third-party rates and said it would open September 1, 2026; confirm it is actually operating. Season + Taste (downtown) and Your Kitchen (Meridian) did not publish a walk-up hourly rate. No verified shared-kitchen listing was found for Eagle or Nampa on this date. Details are in [kitchens.md](kitchens.md).
- **Biggest unknowns for Charles to close by phone:** (1) CDH classification of a private office trial versus a public-event TFE versus catering versus a mobile unit; (2) whether oatmeal may be cooked at home under the 2026 Act if it is packaged and not served on-site, versus cooked in a licensed kitchen; (3) City of Boise classification of a portable private-property breakfast counter; (4) current kitchen availability, minimums, insurance limits, and early-morning hours.

## What Charles should do next

1. Call Central District Health with the script in the [one-day trial checklist](one-day-trial-checklist.md). Get a written or emailed classification if they will provide one. This is the first call.
2. Only after you know the trial type, call the City Clerk (and Planning if the site is a private office lot). Call ACHD only if any part of the setup sits in a sidewalk, curb, or other public right-of-way.
3. Call two or three kitchens (Gina's, Osage, and Flava or The Commons) with the script in [call-scripts.md](call-scripts.md). Ask about 4:00 to 6:00 a.m. access, minimum hours, insurance, and whether they will sign a CDH commissary agreement.
4. Ask your insurance agent for general liability and product liability, and for any extra-insured wording a kitchen, market, or host will require. Do not buy a policy until CDH and the host say what they need.
5. Do not file with the Idaho Secretary of State or the Tax Commission until you have chosen an entity and a name. The question lists are in [call-scripts.md](call-scripts.md). This scout did not file anything.
