## Script

> Hello, my name is Charles Warloe. I am planning a possible one-day trial of a small oatmeal breakfast service in the Boise area, under the working name Oats, Etc. I am not operating yet. I need to know which license, if any, I need, and whether I must use a licensed kitchen.
>
> Here is the product: plain cooked oatmeal, finished with toppings such as brown sugar, cinnamon, raisins, maple, nuts, and possibly an apple topping. No eggs on this trial. I would hold the oatmeal hot, at or above 135 degrees if that is what you require.
>
> I want you to classify several possible one-day setups, because I have not locked the site yet:
> 1. A paid one-day booth at a public event or farmers market.
> 2. A paid one-morning stand on a private office property, about 6:30 to 9:30 a.m., selling bowls to employees or walk-up buyers, using a portable table and electric warmers, with the property owner's permission.
> 3. An employer-paid drop-off of preordered bowls, with no walk-up selling.
> 4. The same office stand, but only free samples and no payment.
>
> Questions I need answered:
> - For each setup, what license do I need? Temporary Food Establishment, mobile, intermittent, catering, permanent, or none?
> - Does a one-day trial need the full permanent food-establishment license and the $100 plan review, or is the one-day temporary license enough?
> - May I cook the oatmeal in my home kitchen under the 2026 Direct-to-Consumer Commerce Act, or does on-site service mean I must cook in a licensed kitchen or on site?
> - If I cook in a shared commercial kitchen and transport the oatmeal in my personal SUV, what is required?
> - What handwashing, water, wastewater, and utensil washing do you require for the smallest legal setup?
> - How far ahead must I apply, what is the current fee, and do I need a Certified Food Protection Manager, the Idaho Food Safety Exam, or the Temporary Food Establishment exam for a one-day license?
> - May I take paid preorders before the license is issued?
> - Can you confirm this in an email I can keep in my files?
>
> My email is __________. The possible site address is __________. The possible date is __________.

## Published fees to confirm on the call (do not treat these as a quote)

From the CDH food establishments page, opened 2026-11-11, VERIFIED-this-session: Temporary Food Establishment $35 for one day, $45 for one event of two or three days, $80 for four days or multiple events; plan review $100; permanent establishment $200; full-service mobile $80 or $100 depending on commissary category; ServSafe course at CDH $150, test only $73; Idaho Food Safety Exam $48 (this does not meet the CFPM requirement). Confirm the fee they assign to your category.

The temporary-event handout, opened 2026-11-11, says to apply at least 30 days before the event, and that the license is issued at the event after a passing inspection. Ask whether that applies to a private office morning as well. That last point is UNVERIFIED.
