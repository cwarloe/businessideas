# Start here: what blocks a one-day trial

**Date:** November 11, 2026.  
**Purpose:** Get one lawful trial morning on the calendar, using the lightest compliant path first. Recurring weekday service, a trailer, and a permanent restaurant license can wait until that path is clear.

This page and [this-week.md](this-week.md) are meant to be read together. This page is the order of work. This-week.md is only what to do in the week of November 11, 2026.

Nothing in this file is a permit or a legal opinion. Regulations change. Central District Health's answer on your exact menu, site, and setup governs.

## The short recommended path

For the current Stage 1 idea (hot oatmeal scooped into a bowl for someone to eat now, at a stand, table, or office counter):

1. **Do not treat Idaho's 2026 homemade-food law as the path for this stand.** The Idaho Direct-to-Consumer Commerce Act replaced the old cottage-food rule on March 20, 2026. "Homemade" does not include food prepared, cooked, or served on-site for immediate consumption, including restaurant-style service, mobile food units, and concession trailers. Source: [Idaho Code 37-203(5)](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/title37/t37ch2/sect37-203/) and the [Idaho Department of Health and Welfare food safety page](https://healthandwelfare.idaho.gov/health-wellness/community-health/food-safety), both opened 2026-11-11, VERIFIED-this-session.
2. **Call Central District Health first.** Ask them to classify the one-day setup you actually might run. Until they do, you do not know which license, kitchen, or city form to use. This is the first gate. A short outline is in [this-week.md](this-week.md). A longer script is already in OE Kitchen's one-day trial checklist in [pull request 4](https://github.com/cwarloe/businessideas/pull/4).
3. **If CDH says a one-day public-event booth is a Temporary Food Establishment, that is the lightest published licensed path found in this session.** CDH's page defines a TFE as a booth or stand that operates for no more than fourteen days in a row in conjunction with a single event or celebration, such as a festival, farmers market, street fair, or parade. The published one-day fee is $35. Apply at least 30 days before the event. The temporary-event handout says the license is issued at the event after a passing inspection. That is still a license. It is not the $200 permanent establishment license, and it is not by itself a license to sell breakfast at an office every weekday. Sources: [CDH food establishments page](https://cdh.idaho.gov/licenses-permits-inspections/food-establishments/) and [Temporary Food Establishment Information (PDF)](https://cdh.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Temporary-Food-Establishment-Information.pdf), opened 2026-11-11, VERIFIED-this-session.
4. **Do not assume a private office one-morning paid stand is that TFE, and do not assume it is homemade.** CDH's published examples are festivals, markets, street fairs, and parades. A hosted office breakfast is not on that list. This classification is **UNVERIFIED**. Ask CDH before you promise a date or take payment. OE Kitchen reached the same conclusion in [pull request 4](https://github.com/cwarloe/businessideas/pull/4).
5. **Do not assume you can cook the oatmeal at home for a walk-up stand.** Ask CDH. A packaged, labeled, direct-to-consumer product that is not served as a meal on site is a different legal box, and it is not the current Stage 1 hero product. See the register, item FOOD-10.
6. **City, tax, insurance, and a kitchen come after the CDH classification, not before.** A CDH license does not replace a City of Boise license if the Clerk says you need one. It does not replace sales tax registration if you need a seller's permit. It does not replace insurance a host, kitchen, or market may require.

## Work in this order (what actually blocks the trial)

Do these in order. Later items often cannot be finished until earlier ones are answered.

1. **Choose which one-day trial you mean**, at least well enough to describe it on the phone: a paid booth at a public event; a paid one-morning stand on a private office lot; an employer-paid drop-off with no walk-up sales; or free samples only, with no payment. You can ask CDH about more than one option on the same call.
2. **Call Central District Health** and get a classification, the application name, the fee they quote, the lead time, whether a licensed kitchen is required, and whether a free tasting or a preorder is treated differently from a paid sale. Ask them to email the answer if they will. Write the answers in a note on your phone or in a Word file on your Windows PC, with the date, the person's name, and the phone number you used.
3. **If CDH requires approved prep, identify a licensed kitchen** and ask whether CDH will accept it. Kitchen leads and call scripts already exist in `notes/oe-kitchen/` ([pull request 4](https://github.com/cwarloe/businessideas/pull/4)). This folder does not repeat that scout.
4. **If the site is in Boise, call the City Clerk** about which city license, if any, applies to that one day. If the site is a private lot, also ask Planning how the City classifies a portable breakfast counter. If any part of the stand, the line, or a power cord might sit on a sidewalk, curb, or planter, call Ada County Highway District. OE Kitchen's checklist in [pull request 4](https://github.com/cwarloe/businessideas/pull/4) already has City and ACHD scripts you can use. If the site is in Meridian, Garden City, Eagle, or Nampa, call that city instead. Nampa food licensing is Southwest District Health, not CDH.
5. **Decide the name and structure you will put on applications**, or decide to stay a sole proprietor and file an assumed business name if you will trade as Oats, Etc. The City of Boise says every business license application must include a current Idaho Secretary of State registration that matches the name on the application. An assumed business name is an SOS registration. It is not a license and not a separate entity. Entity choice (sole proprietor versus LLC) is a lawyer and accountant question because this is a food business. Do not file until you have chosen.
6. **Ask the Idaho State Tax Commission** whether this one-day trial needs a temporary seller's permit, a regular seller's permit, or (only if you are a sole-proprietor Idaho resident and meet every test) the small seller exemption. Applying for a seller's permit is published as free. A recurring Stage 1 that could exceed $5,000 in a year should not be planned around the small seller exemption. Tax Commission phone published on their permits page: (208) 334-7660 or 1-800-972-7660.
7. **Ask your insurance agent** for general liability and product liability, and for any additional-insured wording a kitchen, host, market, or city will require. Do not buy a policy until you know what they require. Premiums are not confirmed in this folder.
8. **Submit the CDH application, if one is required, at least 30 days before the trial.** Use the Accela portal CDH publishes: [https://cdh.idaho.gov/accela/](https://cdh.idaho.gov/accela/). Do not advertise a paid date until the application is in and CDH has not objected to the date.
9. **Then** complete the city license (if required), tax permit (if required), kitchen booking, insurance certificate, food-safety training CDH said you need, and a simple sales-and-temperature record plan.

## What can wait until after the one-day path is clear

These matter later. They should not delay the CDH call, and most should not be bought or filed this week.

- Forming an LLC, unless your lawyer, accountant, insurer, kitchen, or the City says you need one before the trial application. A sole proprietor plus an assumed business name is the lightest SOS path published for using a trade name, but it is not a liability shield. Ask a professional.
- The $200 permanent food-establishment license, the $100 plan review, and a mobile-unit license, unless CDH says the trial needs one of those instead of a TFE.
- A trailer, a specialized cart, or other equipment purchases. The research archive already says not to buy these until demand and a legal site are proven.
- Recurring weekday office service, lunch, and a second site.
- Idaho unemployment insurance, income-tax withholding, and (in most cases) workers' compensation, unless you hire employees or a professional tells you that you are a covered employer. Stage 1 is described as one operator and no paid staff.
- Greater Boise Auditorium District tax. The Tax Commission says that tax applies to short-term lodging, not to oatmeal sales. Source: [Auditorium District Taxes](https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/taxes-on-sales/aud/), opened 2026-11-11, VERIFIED-this-session.
- Trademark registration and a professional name clearance. They matter before you spend money on a brand, but they do not have to be finished before you call CDH. Do not treat a working name as legally cleared.
- A home-occupation permit, unless you will use a Boise home as an office or (only if CDH allows it) as a production site. A home-occupation review does not legalize a walk-up oatmeal restaurant in a residential driveway.

## Timing, so you are not surprised

If CDH requires a TFE or another license that must be submitted 30 days ahead, a paid trial is not a next-week project. If you complete the classification call this week and apply in late November 2026, the earliest 30-day date is mid to late December 2026. Holidays may make that harder. Do not pick a date until CDH and, if needed, the event or host agree.
