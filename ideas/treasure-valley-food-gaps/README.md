## Why this exploration exists

After researching **Oats, Etc.** (grain bowls and a portable office breakfast pilot) and the **fudge sharing/gifting** concept, Charles broadened the question:

> In Boise and the Treasure Valley, which food needs, occasions or niches appear underserved—and which could become profitable, reasonably low-risk businesses?

Possible channels include weekday workplace service, recurring office orders, weddings, private events, gifting, markets, retail partnerships and catering. The question is not limited to breakfast or to a mobile stand.

**Critical additional requirement added during the conversation:** Evaluate **the possibility of profit**, not mere novelty. Exclude or downgrade concepts with heavy capital, very low contribution margin, excessive owner labor, high food waste, hard-to-obtain locations, intense regulatory complexity or weak repeat demand.

The idea is to compare opportunity candidates before creating name/design/site assets. Charles also wanted a prompt usable in **both ChatGPT and Gemini** to compare independent research. The exact previously delivered prompt and any resulting research report are **not verified in this repository**; the prompt below is a reconstruction aligned with the stated goal, not a verbatim archive.

## Reusable research prompt (reconstructed, not verbatim)

> Conduct a source-based market-opportunity investigation of Boise and the broader Treasure Valley (including Meridian, Eagle, Garden City, Nampa and Caldwell). Identify unmet or poorly served customer jobs in prepared food, specialty products, gifting, office breakfast/lunch, catering, weddings/events and other promising occasions. Do not assume that absence of a business proves demand.
>
> Use dated, linked evidence from customer reviews/questions, local business menus and service offerings, local market/event calendars, employer/customer segments and primary government sources. Distinguish *documented customer complaints or demand* from inferred whitespace and brainstorming. Seek counterevidence and substitutes.
>
> Rank candidate opportunities by likely purchase frequency, reachable customer segment, willingness to pay, distribution options, competitor quality, differentiability, pilotability, capital required, recurring overhead, per-unit contribution margin, owner labor, seasonality, food safety/permitting, waste, operational complexity and downside risk.
>
> Provide at least two viable delivery models per top candidate, including the leanest (e.g., approved shared kitchen + preorder/catering + transport) when realistic. Show indicative unit-economics formulas and scenarios, but leave unknown costs, conversion rates and permit answers explicitly unverified rather than inserting confident fake numbers. Compare startup cost, possible gross margin, likely operator hours and break-even. Keep cash profit separate from earnings after owner time.
>
> Finish with a ranked shortlist of 5–10 opportunities, the strongest evidence and counterargument for each, the three most promising cheap experiments, questions for regulators/site hosts and a source register with URLs and check dates. State what must be true for each idea to produce worthwhile *profit*. Exclude generic business platitudes and avoid treating a website poll as purchase validation.
