# Milk Bar — Research Brief v1.0

**Prepared:** November 18, 2026 · **Research not started.**  
**Owner:** Charles Warloe · **Initial geography:** Boise/Treasure Valley, Idaho  
**Companion:** [Concept and decisions](README.md)

## Research objective

Determine whether a quick-service specialty milk counter with a small tasting area could satisfy a **reachable, paying customer need** and earn an attractive **profit per owner-hour**, and whether a counter, hosted pop-up, partner location or event model is the best way to test it.

The concept combines two deliberately competing appeals:

- **Discovery:** taste and compare varieties of dairy and plant-based milk.
- **Enjoyment:** buy milk-forward or indulgent prepared milk drinks repeatedly.

**The task is to test, not confirm, the idea.** Do not mistake positive novelty reactions for sustainable paid demand.

## Hypotheses to investigate

1. There are customers who will pay for a milk tasting flight at a price covering its real costs.
2. Some people will repeatedly buy distinctive milk-centered drinks instead of existing substitutes.
3. Offering both experiences creates more value than operating them separately.
4. A small menu using cow, oat, soy and rice bases can be made consistently with acceptable waste and preparation time.
5. A low-capital channel can test demand before a dedicated storefront.
6. One plausible model can generate an acceptable return **after** owner time, fixed costs and capital risk.

Target audience is intentionally open. Compare at least these *occasions*: afternoon treat, social/date outing, family activity, dairy-alternative discovery, workplace service, and private event.

## Round 1 — Market, differentiation and customer evidence (run first)

### Questions

- Which milk-focused bars, specialty dairy/non-dairy drink counters, tasting concepts or relevant analogs operate now in the US or abroad? Which have closed or visibly pivoted, and what evidence explains why?
- What is genuinely different from boba tea, smoothie/milkshake shops, coffeehouses offering alternative milks, dairy farm stores, dessert cafés, cereal bars, grocery products and bottled flavored milk?
- What does the customer *buy*: a new experience, a treat, a healthy-feeling identity, a dietary substitute, a repeat drink, or an event? Avoid imposing health claims.
- Which customer segments are reachable in Boise/Treasure Valley, and what *direct* behavioral or complaint evidence suggests an underserved need? Could lack of a dedicated milk bar reflect lack of demand?
- Are tasting flights more compelling than signature drinks, or does each appeal to a different occasion?
- Is there defensible willingness to pay beyond grocery milk prices? Distinguish actual posted menus, verified purchases, interviews and speculation.
- What would make a low-capital experiment credible, legal, measurable and easy to abandon?

### Required evidence

- Date-checked links to actual business sites/menus, filings or other authoritative current references. Prioritize **primary sources** for operating models, published prices, official data, permits and IP.
- Include **local substitutes and alternatives**, not just businesses literally named "Milk Bar." List business/location and product category; don't invent businesses.
- Note any known major commercial uses of the words "Milk Bar" and flag brand clearance; do not render a legal conclusion.
- Keep competitor closures, revenue, profitability, customer frequency and sales volumes **UNKNOWN** unless genuinely supported by evidence.
- For user-generated reviews/social posts, label small-sample/self-selection bias and do not generalize to a population.
- Distinguish `VERIFIED CURRENT`, `SOURCE-REPORTED`, `INFERENCE`, `HYPOTHESIS`, `NOT FOUND`, and `NEEDS FIELD TEST`.

### Deliverables

1. **Executive verdict**: best argument *for* and *against* the concept; strongest unresolved question; tentative next action, not a launch recommendation.
2. **Analog comparison** of roughly 8–12 relevant ventures, plus a grounded Treasure Valley alternatives table. Show what they sell, customer occasion, published price (if available), format, differentiator, source/date and important limitation.
3. **Three candidate customer segments/occasions** ranked by evidence strength and reachability, with a specific acquisition channel and competing substitutes.
4. **Two-appeal positioning test design**, comparing "Discover Your Favorite Milk" and "Your New Favorite Drink" using comparable menu, pricing, time/traffic and actual transaction outcomes.
5. **Low-capital model screen** comparing pop-up, hosted counter, dedicated counter, and event tasting; include no unsupported cost or revenue projections.
6. **Top ten unknowns / fatal assumptions**, with cheapest decisive evidence and a stop/pivot threshold.
7. **Source register** (URL, publisher, published/updated date if available, access date, claim, caveat). Explicitly identify unsupported assertions and conflicts.

## Round 2 — Product, regulation and profitability (only after review of Round 1)

Research and price, using supplier quotes or dated posted sources:

- Dairy and plant-based supplier channels; packaged volume; variations in ingredients and labeling.
- Opened-product storage, shelf life, cold chain, transport, permitted service and required sinks/sanitation. Confirm with **Central District Health and relevant local authorities** for each proposed format; the report is not an authorization or legal opinion.
- Allergy handling and cross-contact, including milk/soy and potential later nut milks.
- Recipe sensory/texture behavior: not every milk base is suitable for every flavor or heating method.
- Equipment, refrigeration, vessels, cleanup, spoilage and prep/throughput in peak periods.
- Contribution per SKU, realistic product mix and breakeven under downside cases.
- Cash operating profit *and* economic profit after valuing owner time. No "gross revenue = profit" shortcuts.
- Storefront versus host/partner versus event delivery with startup cash, recurring costs, capacity and downside.

**Output:** receipt/quote-ready workbook, formulas and assumptions register. Do not fill unverified costs with invented facts.

## Round 3 — Field validation design (after Round 2)

Design an appropriately approved small paid pilot with:

- Defined audience, traffic denominator and sampling limitations.
- A pre-declared purchase/willingness-to-pay threshold—not just free poll votes.
- Separate measures: people exposed, visitors, transactions, flight vs drink sales, conversion from flight to full drink, repeat purchases, revenue, contribution, waste, queue duration, owner hours.
- Controlled comparison of discovery-led and beverage-led invitations, with comparable hours, location, price and menu.
- Safety, legal classification, host permission and privacy requirements handled **before** any serving/payment.
- Explicit abandon/pivot/continue rules and a postmortem.
