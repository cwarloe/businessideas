# Milk Bar · next decision brief

Prepared November 18, 2026. **Proposed discovery work. No interviews or test sales have been run for this brief.** Charles remains the decision owner. Charles or a named successor would conduct conversations and record evidence. The initial sample and timebox below are suggested defaults.

## Decision to unlock

Which purchase occasion gives a reachable customer a reason to buy milk from this business?

The owner has no strong preference for a cup, a take-home container, pickup or delivery. Preserve cold and warm options. Oatmeal can be another product later, but this round asks why the customer would buy milk itself.

| Offer being considered | Behavior worth understanding | Likely next operating evidence if buyers support it |
|---|---|---|
| A cup to drink now, cold or warm | Last milk drink purchased away from home, what prompted it, alternatives and repeat purchase | A suitable host, process, one product's quality and price, service time |
| A supplier-sealed container to take home | Current milk source, usual pack/price, missed purchases and willingness to change routine | Supplier resale terms, stock life, pickup access and permitted handling |
| A clustered milk delivery | Current delivery use or a recurring difficulty that delivery would solve | Orders per stop, route time, total delivery charge and supplier/authority requirements |

## Three uncertainties in priority order

1. **Reason to switch.** An existing grocery, dairy store, delivery provider or other drink may already satisfy the customer. Learn what the last purchase actually did for them.
2. **Reach and concentration.** Enough suitable buyers must be present at the stop or close together on a route. Learn where and when the occasion occurs before selecting a site.
3. **Return after full delivery costs.** A small per-sale margin can disappear into access charges, packaging, cold storage or travel. Cost the format supported by discovery before comparing it with the cup/flight workbook.

## First discovery round

**Suggested timebox** — two total hours for recruitment, conversations and notes, then review. Aim for four relevant conversations if reachable within that cap, including someone who recently bought a milk drink away from home and someone who buys household milk. Treat each as evidence about that person's occasion. Small counts will rarely settle the market choice.

**Recruitment route** — prospective customers who can describe recent purchases. Start with accessible adults, record existing relationships, and include people outside the immediate friend circle where practical. Approach a venue's patrons only with appropriate site permission. Keep identities private.

**Proposed invitation**

> I’m exploring a small business selling milk for immediate drinking, pickup or delivery. Could I ask about the last time you bought milk, where you got it, and what led you to choose it?

1. What was your most recent milk purchase? Was it a household container or an individual drink?
2. What exact product, size and price did you choose? Where and when did you buy it?
3. Who was it for, and what prompted the purchase?
4. What else did you consider, including a different drink or an existing delivery service?
5. What was inconvenient or disappointing, if anything? What already works well?
6. Have you bought it again? When is the next likely purchase?
7. After understanding their behavior, describe the relevant proposed format and ask what would make it useful or unsuitable. If warmth comes up, ask when they last purchased warm milk and what they chose. If delivery comes up, ask what recurring charge and delivery window would fit the actual household or office routine.

## Decide from the evidence

| Finding | Next action |
|---|---|
| A specific immediate-drinking occasion appears, with an accessible place and reason to switch | Price one simple product and the exact host arrangement. |
| A take-home need appears, with a repeatable pickup routine | Price supplier-sealed resale and pickup separately. |
| A recurring delivery need appears among nearby buyers | Map anonymized stops, frequency and order size, then price the route. |
| Existing alternatives meet the need and no actionable difference emerges | Park that segment or identify a specific different occasion worth investigating. |
| Answers are mixed, hypothetical or limited by recruitment | Mark inconclusive, keep options open and name the one missing observation. |

Record invitations, qualified conversations, contradictions, prices recalled and evidence limits in the [run record](../../../playbook/discovery-and-test-run-template.md). Label stated interest. A repeat purchase from an incumbent establishes an existing habit, and a later purchase from this business would test switching.

## Gate before a paid test

Choose the format to test, product/source, actual price, audience, site, complete operating costs and all labor. Set cash/time limits, thresholds and an opportunity to buy again before inviting purchases. The existing cup/flight calculator and cold-drink inquiry are starting records for that process. Hot service, supplier-sealed resale and delivery need their own cost/operating questions answered. For the chosen activity, identify the operator and obtain the relevant supplier, host and authority determinations.

**Review trigger** — after the suggested two-hour round, or sooner when evidence changes the next decision. Set a calendar date when the person doing the work is assigned.
