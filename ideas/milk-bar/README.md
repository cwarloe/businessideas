# Milk Bar — concept and decision record

**Created:** November 18, 2026  
**Stage:** Round 1 market research complete, awaiting owner review  
**Status:** HYPOTHESIS — no product sold, no business name cleared, no food-service approval, no storefront or equipment selected  
**Working label:** "Milk Bar" describes the category, **not** a trademark-cleared name  
**Initial research geography:** Boise/Treasure Valley, Idaho, with international analogs for comparison

## One-sentence concept

A **quick-service specialty milk counter with a small tasting area**, offering dairy and plant-based milks both simply served and as excellent signature drinks. Discovery flights invite experimentation; repeat-worthy prepared beverages provide a potential recurring revenue stream.

Two distinct appeals are deliberately being tested **without assuming a winner**:

1. **Discovery:** "Try different milks and find your favorite."
2. **Enjoyment:** "Come back for a drink you love."

A flight-to-full-drink or flight-to-repeat-customer pathway is a hypothesis, not evidence.

## Customer experience

| Visitor | What they do | Operational intent |
|---|---|---|
| Express customer | Choose milk-forward or signature drink, pay, pick up, go | Speed and consistent drink quality |
| Discovery customer | Purchase labeled flight; use small standing tasting ledge; compare varieties; optionally order a full drink | Interesting experience without blocking checkout |
| Group/event customer (optional model) | Book a staffed or self-guided tasting experience | Test group economics separately |

Proposed initial counter zones: express order/pickup, **2–4-person standing tasting ledge**, and shared back-of-counter refrigeration/preparation/cleaning facilities. Dimensions, sink requirements, separations and permitted equipment are **not specified** until regulatory and site research.

A simple paper "Milk Passport" may support tasting notes (flavor, creaminess, sweetness, preference). It is **not** a customer-data system and must not be used as sales validation by itself.

## Menu architecture — hypotheses, not final products

| Family | Proposed starting offer | Purpose | Major unknown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pure & Simple | Plain cow, oat, soy or rice milk; cold initially | Let milk differences be the attraction | Will buyers pay for a plain glass? |
| Discovery | Four-base plain tasting flight; **flavored mini-flight** as a separate idea to test | Acquire customers and reveal preferences | Price acceptance, assembly time, waste |
| Signature drinks | Chocolate, vanilla-cinnamon, strawberry, plus a possible rotating flavor | Encourage repeat orders | Sensory quality and cost by compatible base |
| Pairings | One simple cookie/brownie via an approved bakery partner | Test higher average order | Adds value or just adds complexity? |

**Starting bases:** cow, oat, soy and rice are research candidates, not a purchasing commitment. Possible later expansion: lactose-free cow, almond, coconut, pea, goat, sheep and others after supply, safety, labeling and demand checks.

**Do not launch with every base × flavor × temperature combination.** Test recipes for taste, texture, consistency and preparation time. Cold service is the simplest initial hypothesis; hot drinks are an option for later testing.

**Scope choice still open:** coffee/espresso, ice cream, shakes and smoothies are currently proposed *outside* the initial milk-centered concept. Charles has not yet explicitly confirmed the exclusion. Treat them as competitors or future extensions, not committed menu items.

## Customer and business hypotheses

| Hypothesis | What would count as evidence? |
|---|---|
| Curious visitors will pay for milk comparison | Purchases of flights at tested prices among a defined reachable audience |
| People will buy milk-centered specialty beverages repeatedly | Paid repeat orders over multiple weeks, not just enthusiastic comments |
| Tasting generates valuable drink customers | Tracked, privacy-conscious conversions from flight to full-size purchase and future repeat |
| One small operation can serve both paths | Measured queue times, assembly labor, equipment/sanitation requirements and throughput |
| Four initial bases are manageable | Supplier package sizes, actual use, opened-product waste and proper storage |
| At least one reachable customer segment supports adequate sales | Defined customer/occasion, traffic denominator, frequency and price evidence |
| At least one format is financially attractive | Contribution and owner-hour profit after genuine recurring costs and capital risk |

Potential customer occasions to compare: afternoon treat, social outing, dairy-alternative exploration, family activity, workplace catering and private events. **No primary target customer has been chosen.**

## Business models to compare

1. **Hosted pop-up/event counter:** low-commitment paid validation at an approved site; event frequency and repeat demand are limitations.
2. **Counter within an existing licensed business:** potential access to foot traffic and facilities; permissions, revenue-share and role boundaries unknown.
3. **Small dedicated counter with tasting ledge:** brand control and repeat service, but rent/staffing and regular traffic need much stronger proof.
4. **Private event / catered tasting bar:** potentially larger grouped tickets; operational, food safety and transport approvals must be researched separately.

No lease, specialized vehicle/trailer, custom bottling operation or multiple-counter rollout should be assumed in early financial models.

## Financial discipline

For each actual menu SKU, calculate:

- **Revenue per order**: real tested price and product mix.
- **Variable cost**: usable milk ounces, flavors, cup/lid/spoons/tasting containers, payment fee, consumables, attributable waste and fulfillment.
- **Contribution** = revenue − variable cost.
- **Fixed/period costs**: legally required kitchen/site/permits/insurance, refrigeration/utilities, software, transport, marketing, staff if used.
- **Owner hours**: sourcing, prep, travel, setup, service, cleaning, admin and marketing; show both cash result and result **after valuing owner time**.
- **Sensitivity**: low/base/high demand, poor product mix, higher waste, different operating days and price acceptance.
- **Capital at risk**: startup equipment, inventory, deposits, resale value and time to recover investment.

Never call gross sales profit. Earlier illustrative price/cost examples from brainstorming were **arithmetic demonstrations, not sourced quotes**. Keep them out of forecasts.

## Key risks and reasons to abandon or pivot

- Milk is perceived as a low-price commodity; a premium may not be accepted.
- Competes with boba, smoothies, coffeehouse non-dairy drinks, milkshakes and ordinary grocery milk, even if those aren't called "milk bars."
- Many opened varieties could mean expensive spoilage and refrigerated storage.
- Flights use multiple small vessels and may be slow at peak demand.
- Dairy/soy/nut allergies, cross-contact, temperature control and food-service authorization may constrain menu and layout.
- Novelty demand may evaporate after one visit.
- A standalone counter may consume too much owner time or fixed overhead for an acceptable return.
- "Milk Bar" as a name requires early trademark/common-law research; label does **not** establish availability.

## Round 1 record and lessons

Read the [market report](research/round-1-market-research.md), [dated source register](research/round-1-sources.md) and original [research brief](research-brief-v1.md).

- A $6 small fresh strawberry milk is already listed in Boise. Any proposed signature has to earn another purchase against that incumbent. [Urban Fox menu](https://urbanfoxidaho.com/menu/)
